\chapter{The Nullspace Method}
\label{chap:nullspace}

\vspace*{\fill}

The nullspace method solves the KKT system~\eqref{eq:kkt-system} in two smaller steps. The
search direction is written as $p = Z\eta$, where the columns of $Z$ span the nullspace of
the constraint matrix. This turns the KKT system into a \textbf{reduced system} with the
reduced Hessian, whose dimension equals the number of degrees of freedom of the current face.
The Lagrange multipliers are then recovered from the remaining equations.

\medskip

For the regularized master problem both steps can be carried out explicitly. The reordering
theorem of the previous chapter gives a nullspace basis that consists of the vectors
$e_j - e_{\rep(j)}$ and of unit vectors for the free surpluses. This basis is
\textbf{never stored}: it is determined by the five sets alone.

\medskip

The central result of this chapter is a \textbf{decoupling} of the reduced system. The
surplus part of the direction is given by an explicit formula, and only an
$|\Nx|\times|\Nx|$ system has to be solved. Its matrix is the \textbf{effective reduced
Hessian} $\Heff = (\Aeff Z_x)^\top(\Aeff Z_x)$, the reduced Hessian of the equality rows
together with the inequality rows whose surplus is active. Finally, the multipliers turn out
to be the reduced costs of the columns and the duals of the inequality rows, available at
almost no cost.

\vspace*{\fill}

\section{The Nullspace Basis}
\label{sec:basis}

Every search direction must satisfy the constraints of the subproblem~\eqref{eq:eqp},
$D_\Wset\,p = 0$. The set of such directions is $\Null(D_\Wset)$, which by \cref{thm:rank} has
dimension $|\Nx|+|\Ns|$. We construct a basis whose vectors are labelled by the indices of
$\Nx$ and $\Ns$.

\begin{theorem}[Nullspace basis] \label{thm:basis}
Under the assumptions of \cref{thm:reordering}, the matrix
\begin{equation} \label{eq:Z-reordered}
Z = Q\tilde Z,\qquad
\tilde Z = \begin{bmatrix}
-C_{\Nx} & 0\\
0 & 0\\
I_{|\Nx|} & 0\\
0 & 0\\
0 & I_{|\Ns|}
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{matrix} \leftarrow\Sset\\ \leftarrow\Wx\\ \leftarrow\Nx\\ \leftarrow\Ws\\ \leftarrow\Ns\end{matrix}
\end{equation}
is a basis of $\Null(D_\Wset)$. In the original order of the variables it is block-diagonal,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:Z-blocks}
Z = \begin{bmatrix} Z_x & 0\\ 0 & Z_s\end{bmatrix},
\qquad Z_x\in\R^{n\times|\Nx|},\quad Z_s\in\R^{m_I\times|\Ns|},
\end{equation}
where $Z_x$ acts on the column weights and $Z_s$ is the selection matrix of the free surpluses
(its columns are the unit vectors $e_i\in\R^{m_I}$, $i\in\Ns$).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Since $QQ^\top = I$, a matrix $Z$ satisfies $D_\Wset Z = 0$ if and only if $\tilde Z = Q^\top Z$
satisfies $(D_\Wset Q)\tilde Z = 0$. We split $\tilde Z$ into the five row blocks of the extended
ordering,
\[
\tilde Z = \bigl[\,Z_\Sset;\; Z_{\Wx};\; Z_{\Nx};\; Z_{\Ws};\; Z_{\Ns}\,\bigr].
\]
With the reordered matrix of \cref{thm:reordering},
\[
(D_\Wset Q)\tilde Z =
\begin{bmatrix}
I_K & C_{\Wx} & C_{\Nx} & 0 & 0\\
0 & I & 0 & 0 & 0\\
0 & 0 & 0 & I & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix} Z_\Sset\\ Z_{\Wx}\\ Z_{\Nx}\\ Z_{\Ws}\\ Z_{\Ns}\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix} Z_\Sset + C_{\Wx}Z_{\Wx} + C_{\Nx}Z_{\Nx}\\ Z_{\Wx}\\ Z_{\Ws}\end{bmatrix}.
\]
This vanishes if and only if $Z_{\Wx} = 0$, $Z_{\Ws} = 0$ and $Z_\Sset = -C_{\Nx}Z_{\Nx}$. The
blocks $Z_{\Nx}$ and $Z_{\Ns}$ can be chosen freely. With $|\Nx| + |\Ns|$ columns, the choice
$Z_{\Nx} = [\,I\;\;0\,]$, $Z_{\Ns} = [\,0\;\;I\,]$ gives~\eqref{eq:Z-reordered}. Its columns are
linearly independent, since each has a $1$ in a position where all others have a $0$, and their
number equals $\dim\Null(D_\Wset)$, so they form a basis. Finally, the first group of columns has
nonzeros only in the column weights ($\Sset$ and $\Nx$), and the second group only in the
surpluses ($\Ns$), which gives the block-diagonal form~\eqref{eq:Z-blocks}.
\end{proof}

\begin{cor}[Explicit nullspace vectors] \label{cor:z-explicit}
The columns of $Z$ are
\begin{equation} \label{eq:z-explicit}
z_j = e_j - e_{\rep(j)}\quad (j\in\Nx),\qquad\qquad z_i = e_{n+i}\quad (i\in\Ns),
\end{equation}
where $e_\ell$ denotes the $\ell$-th unit vector in $\R^{n+m_I}$.
\end{cor}

\begin{proof}
Column $j\in\Nx$ of $\tilde Z$ has a $1$ in the position of $j$ (from $Z_{\Nx} = I$) and the
vector $-C_{\Nx}e_j$ in the block of the representatives. Since column $j$ of $C$ is
$e_{\psi(j)}$, this vector is $-e_{\psi(j)}$: a single $-1$ in position $\psi(j)$ of the list
$\Sset$, which is the representative $S_{\psi(j)} = \rep(j)$. So $z_j = e_j - e_{\rep(j)}$.
Column $i\in\Ns$ has a single $1$ in the position of surplus $i$, i.e.\ $z_i = e_{n+i}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{note}
The column part of $z_j$ is the vector $e_j - e_{\rep(j)}\in\R^n$, which is column $j$ of
$Z_x$. To keep the notation light we use the symbol $z_j$ also for this vector, whenever only
the column weights are involved.
\end{note}

\Cref{cor:z-explicit} shows that $Z$ is completely determined by the extended ordering: by
the sets $\Nx$, $\Ns$ and the representative mapping. It therefore never has to be formed.
The same holds for the products of the linking matrix with the nullspace vectors, which
appear in the reduced Hessian below. Since column $j$ of $\Abar$ is $a_j$ and column $n+i$ is
$-e_i^{(I)}$, the unit vector of inequality row $i$ in $\R^m$,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:AZ-columns}
\Abar z_j = a_j - a_{\rep(j)} =: u_j\quad(j\in\Nx),\qquad
\Abar z_i = -e_i^{(I)}\quad(i\in\Ns).
\end{equation}


\section{The Reduced KKT System}
\label{sec:reduced}

\subsection{The reduced system}

Every direction that satisfies the constraints of~\eqref{eq:eqp} has the form
$p = Z\eta$ with $\eta = [\eta_x;\eta_s]\in\R^{|\Nx|+|\Ns|}$. Inserting $p = Z\eta$ into the
objective of~\eqref{eq:eqp} gives
\[
\phi(\eta) = \tfrac{1}{2\delta}\,\eta^\top Z^\top\Abar^\top\Abar Z\,\eta + (Z^\top g)^\top\eta,
\]
an unconstrained quadratic in $\eta$. Its minimizer solves the \textbf{reduced KKT system}
\begin{equation} \label{eq:reduced-system}
\frac{1}{\delta}\,Z^\top\Abar^\top\Abar Z\,\eta = -Z^\top g .
\end{equation}
(Equivalently, it is the first block row of the KKT system~\eqref{eq:kkt-system} multiplied by
$Z^\top$, which removes the multiplier term because $Z^\top D_\Wset^\top = (D_\Wset Z)^\top = 0$.)
We call $\Hbar = Z^\top\Abar^\top\Abar Z$ the (unscaled) \textbf{reduced Hessian}, and
$Z^\top g$ the \textbf{reduced gradient}. Multiplied by $\delta$, the system reads
$\Hbar\eta = -\delta Z^\top g$, so $\delta$ only appears on the right-hand side.

\subsection{Block structure}

To exhibit the structure of $\Hbar$, we introduce the products of the linking matrices with
the column part of the basis,
\[
B_E := A_E Z_x\in\R^{m_E\times|\Nx|},\qquad B_I := A_I Z_x\in\R^{m_I\times|\Nx|},
\]
and split the rows of $B_I$ and of $\pi_I$ according to the status of the surplus of each row:
\[
B_\Wset := W_s B_I,\quad \pi_\Wset := W_s\pi_I \qquad\text{(rows with an active surplus)},
\]
\[
B_\mathcal{N} := Z_s^\top B_I,\quad \pi_\mathcal{N} := Z_s^\top\pi_I \qquad\text{(rows with a free surplus)}.
\]
Since every inequality row belongs to exactly one of the two groups, the two selections
together give the identity, $W_s^\top W_s + Z_sZ_s^\top = I$, and therefore
\begin{equation} \label{eq:BI-split}
B_I^\top B_I = B_I^\top\bigl(W_s^\top W_s + Z_sZ_s^\top\bigr)B_I
= B_\Wset^\top B_\Wset + B_\mathcal{N}^\top B_\mathcal{N} .
\end{equation}

\begin{lemma}[Blocks of the reduced system] \label{lem:reduced-blocks}
With the block-diagonal basis~\eqref{eq:Z-blocks},
\[
\Abar Z = \begin{bmatrix} B_E & 0\\ B_I & -Z_s\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\Hbar = \begin{bmatrix} H & -B_\mathcal{N}^\top\\ -B_\mathcal{N} & I\end{bmatrix},\qquad
Z^\top g = \begin{bmatrix} Z_x^\top g_x\\ \pi_\mathcal{N}\end{bmatrix},
\]
where $H = B_E^\top B_E + B_I^\top B_I = (AZ_x)^\top(AZ_x)$ is the reduced Hessian of the column
weights with all linking rows.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Multiplying the blocks,
\[
\Abar Z = \begin{bmatrix} A_E & 0\\ A_I & -I\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix} Z_x & 0\\ 0 & Z_s\end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} A_EZ_x & 0\\ A_IZ_x & -Z_s\end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix} B_E & 0\\ B_I & -Z_s\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Then $\Hbar = (\Abar Z)^\top(\Abar Z)$ has the diagonal blocks $B_E^\top B_E + B_I^\top B_I = H$
and $Z_s^\top Z_s = I$, and the off-diagonal block $-B_I^\top Z_s = -(Z_s^\top B_I)^\top = -B_\mathcal{N}^\top$.
Finally, with $g = [g_x; g_s]$ and $g_s = \pi_I$ by~\eqref{eq:gradient-split},
$Z^\top g = [\,Z_x^\top g_x;\; Z_s^\top\pi_I\,] = [\,Z_x^\top g_x;\; \pi_\mathcal{N}\,]$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Decoupling and the effective reduced Hessian}

The identity block in the lower right corner of $\Hbar$ allows us to eliminate the surplus
part of the direction.

\begin{theorem}[Decoupling of the reduced system] \label{thm:decoupling}
The reduced system~\eqref{eq:reduced-system} is equivalent to
\begin{align}
\Heff\,\eta_x &= -\delta\,Z_x^\top\bigl(c - A_E^\top\pi_E - A_I^\top W_s^\top\pi_\Wset\bigr),
\label{eq:eff-system}\\[3pt]
\eta_s &= B_\mathcal{N}\,\eta_x - \delta\,\pi_\mathcal{N},
\label{eq:surplus-direction}
\end{align}
with the \textbf{effective reduced Hessian}
\begin{equation} \label{eq:Heff}
\Heff := H - B_\mathcal{N}^\top B_\mathcal{N} = B_E^\top B_E + B_\Wset^\top B_\Wset .
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Multiplied by $\delta$, the reduced system is $\Hbar\eta = -\delta Z^\top g$. With the blocks
of \cref{lem:reduced-blocks} it consists of the two equations
\[
H\eta_x - B_\mathcal{N}^\top\eta_s = -\delta Z_x^\top g_x,\qquad
-B_\mathcal{N}\eta_x + \eta_s = -\delta\pi_\mathcal{N}.
\]
The second equation, solved for $\eta_s$, is~\eqref{eq:surplus-direction}. Substituting it into
the first equation gives
\[
H\eta_x - B_\mathcal{N}^\top\bigl(B_\mathcal{N}\eta_x - \delta\pi_\mathcal{N}\bigr) = -\delta Z_x^\top g_x
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\bigl(H - B_\mathcal{N}^\top B_\mathcal{N}\bigr)\eta_x = -\delta\bigl(Z_x^\top g_x + B_\mathcal{N}^\top\pi_\mathcal{N}\bigr).
\]
The matrix on the left is $\Heff$; its second expression in~\eqref{eq:Heff} follows from
$H = B_E^\top B_E + B_I^\top B_I$ and~\eqref{eq:BI-split}. For the right-hand side, insert
$g_x = c - A_E^\top\pi_E - A_I^\top\pi_I$ from~\eqref{eq:gradient-split}. Its last term
contributes $Z_x^\top A_I^\top\pi_I = B_I^\top\pi_I$, which splits like~\eqref{eq:BI-split} as
$B_I^\top\pi_I = B_\Wset^\top\pi_\Wset + B_\mathcal{N}^\top\pi_\mathcal{N}$. Hence
\[
Z_x^\top g_x + B_\mathcal{N}^\top\pi_\mathcal{N}
= Z_x^\top c - B_E^\top\pi_E - B_\Wset^\top\pi_\Wset - B_\mathcal{N}^\top\pi_\mathcal{N} + B_\mathcal{N}^\top\pi_\mathcal{N}
= Z_x^\top\bigl(c - A_E^\top\pi_E - A_I^\top W_s^\top\pi_\Wset\bigr),
\]
using $B_E^\top = Z_x^\top A_E^\top$ and $B_\Wset^\top = Z_x^\top A_I^\top W_s^\top$. This
is~\eqref{eq:eff-system}. The steps can be reversed, so the systems are equivalent.
\end{proof}

In linear algebra terms, $\Heff$ is the Schur complement of the identity block in $\Hbar$
(\cref{fig:schur}). Only an $|\Nx|\times|\Nx|$ system has to be solved; the surplus
part~\eqref{eq:surplus-direction} costs one sparse matrix--vector product. The following
corollary gives the interpretation that the rest of this work builds on.

\begin{cor}[Effective linking matrix] \label{cor:Aeff}
Define the \textbf{effective linking matrix} and the corresponding duals
\[
\Aeff = \begin{bmatrix} A_E\\ W_sA_I\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\pieff = \begin{bmatrix}\pi_E\\ \pi_\Wset\end{bmatrix},
\]
i.e.\ the equality rows together with the inequality rows whose surplus is active. Then
\begin{equation} \label{eq:Heff-Aeff}
\Heff = (\Aeff Z_x)^\top(\Aeff Z_x),\qquad
\Heff\,\eta_x = -\delta\,Z_x^\top\bigl(c - \Aeff^\top\pieff\bigr).
\end{equation}
This is the reduced system of a master problem with equality linking rows only, whose
linking rows are the rows of $\Aeff$.
\end{cor}

\begin{proof}
Stacking the rows, $\Aeff Z_x = [\,A_EZ_x;\; W_sA_IZ_x\,] = [\,B_E;\; B_\Wset\,]$, so
$(\Aeff Z_x)^\top(\Aeff Z_x) = B_E^\top B_E + B_\Wset^\top B_\Wset = \Heff$ by~\eqref{eq:Heff}.
Similarly $\Aeff^\top\pieff = A_E^\top\pi_E + A_I^\top W_s^\top\pi_\Wset$, so the right-hand side
of~\eqref{eq:Heff-Aeff} is that of~\eqref{eq:eff-system}. For a problem without inequality rows,
\cref{thm:decoupling} gives $\Heff = B_E^\top B_E$ and the right-hand side
$-\delta Z_x^\top(c - A_E^\top\pi_E)$; replacing $A_E$ by $\Aeff$ gives~\eqref{eq:Heff-Aeff}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Interpretation] \label{rem:Aeff-interpretation}
An inequality row with a \emph{free} surplus does not restrict the column weights: any
change of its residual is absorbed by the surplus, so the row contributes neither curvature
nor dual pressure to the column part of the problem. An inequality row with an \emph{active}
surplus behaves like an equality row. The column part of the method is therefore the
method for equality rows, applied to a linking matrix $\Aeff$ whose rows change with the
surplus working set. Without inequality rows, or with all surpluses active, $\Aeff = A$ and
$\Heff = H$.
\end{remark}

\begin{cor}[Positive definiteness and dimension] \label{cor:dimension}
$\Hbar$ is positive definite if and only if $\Heff$ is, i.e.\ if and only if $\Aeff Z_x$ has
full column rank. In this case
\[
|\Nx|\;\le\;\rank(\Aeff)\;\le\;m_E + |\Ws|,\qquad\text{and hence}\qquad |\Nx| + |\Ns|\le m .
\]
\end{cor}

\begin{proof}
Completing the square with the blocks of \cref{lem:reduced-blocks} gives, for every
$\eta = [\eta_x;\eta_s]$,
\[
\eta^\top\Hbar\eta = \eta_x^\top H\eta_x - 2\eta_s^\top B_\mathcal{N}\eta_x + \eta_s^\top\eta_s
= \eta_x^\top\Heff\eta_x + \lVert\eta_s - B_\mathcal{N}\eta_x\rVert^2 .
\]
If $\Heff$ is positive definite, the right-hand side is positive unless $\eta_x = 0$ and then
$\eta_s = B_\mathcal{N}\eta_x = 0$, so $\Hbar$ is positive definite. Conversely, choosing
$\eta_s = B_\mathcal{N}\eta_x$ shows $\eta_x^\top\Heff\eta_x = \eta^\top\Hbar\eta$, so $\Heff$ is
positive definite if $\Hbar$ is. By \cref{cor:Aeff}, $\Heff$ is a Gram matrix,
$\eta_x^\top\Heff\eta_x = \lVert\Aeff Z_x\eta_x\rVert^2$, which is positive for all
$\eta_x\ne0$ if and only if $\Aeff Z_x$ has full column rank. Full column rank requires
$|\Nx|\le\rank(\Aeff Z_x)\le\rank(\Aeff)$, and $\Aeff$ has $m_E+|\Ws|$ rows. Adding
$|\Ns| = m_I - |\Ws|$ to both sides gives $|\Nx| + |\Ns|\le m_E + m_I = m$.
\end{proof}

\Cref{cor:dimension} is the reason the method scales: the system that has to be factorized
never exceeds the number of linking rows, \emph{however many columns are generated}.

\subsection{The search direction}

With $\eta$ from \cref{thm:decoupling}, the direction $p = Z\eta = \sum_{j\in\Nx}\eta_jz_j +
\sum_{i\in\Ns}\eta_iz_i$ is obtained by inserting the explicit vectors~\eqref{eq:z-explicit}.
Every $z_j = e_j - e_{\rep(j)}$ adds $\eta_j$ to column $j$ and subtracts it from the
representative of its block, and every $z_i$ moves surplus $i$. Hence
\begin{equation} \label{eq:direction-components}
p_j = \eta_j\;(j\in\Nx),\qquad
p_{S_k} = -\!\!\sum_{j\in\Nx,\,\psi(j) = k}\!\!\eta_j\;(k = 1,\dots,K),\qquad
(p_s)_i = (A_Ip_x)_i - \delta\,\pi_i\;(i\in\Ns),
\end{equation}
and $p$ is zero on $\Wx$ and $\Ws$. For the surplus component we used~\eqref{eq:surplus-direction}
together with $B_\mathcal{N}\eta_x = Z_s^\top A_I Z_x\eta_x = Z_s^\top A_I p_x$, i.e.\
$(B_\mathcal{N}\eta_x)_i = (A_Ip_x)_i$. The column part $p_x$ and the vector $A_Ip_x$ only involve
the columns in $\Sset\cup\Nx$.

\begin{prop}[Full step] \label{prop:full-step}
If the ratio test returns $\alpha = 1$, the new iterate $y_k+p$ is a subspace minimizer, i.e.\
the reduced gradient $Z^\top\nabla f(y_k+p)$ vanishes.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
The gradient of $f$ is affine, $\nabla f(y_k + p) = g + \frac{1}{\delta}\Abar^\top\Abar p$. With
$p = Z\eta$,
\[
Z^\top\nabla f(y_k+p) = Z^\top g + \tfrac{1}{\delta}Z^\top\Abar^\top\Abar Z\,\eta
= Z^\top g + \tfrac{1}{\delta}\Hbar\eta = 0
\]
by the reduced system~\eqref{eq:reduced-system}.
\end{proof}

After a full step the method can therefore skip the next direction computation and test the
multipliers directly. The next proposition shows how the free surpluses act on the duals.

\begin{prop}[Duals of free-surplus rows] \label{prop:free-duals}
A step $y\gets y + \alpha p$ changes the dual of every row with a free surplus as
\[
\pi_i\;\gets\;(1-\alpha)\,\pi_i\qquad(i\in\Ns).
\]
In particular $\pi_i = 0$ on these rows after a full step, and at every subspace minimizer.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
By the definition $\pi(y) = \pihat + \frac{1}{\delta}(b - \Abar y)$, the step changes $\pi$ by
$-\frac{\alpha}{\delta}\Abar p$. Row $i$ of $\Abar p$, for an inequality row, is
$(A_Ip_x)_i - (p_s)_i$. For $i\in\Ns$, \eqref{eq:direction-components} gives
$(p_s)_i = (A_Ip_x)_i - \delta\pi_i$, so $(\Abar p)_i = \delta\pi_i$ and the new dual is
$\pi_i - \frac{\alpha}{\delta}\delta\pi_i = (1-\alpha)\pi_i$. At a subspace minimizer the
reduced gradient vanishes, and its component for $i\in\Ns$ is $\pi_i$ (\cref{lem:reduced-blocks}).
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Independence of $\delta$ and $\pihat$] \label{rem:factor-independent}
$\Heff = (\Aeff Z_x)^\top(\Aeff Z_x)$ depends only on the columns and the working set, not on
$\delta$, $\pihat$ or the iterate. A change of the stability center or of $\delta$ only changes
the right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:eff-system}. This is what makes the hot start of
\cref{sec:hot-start} possible.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Implicit evaluation}

Neither $Z$, nor $\Aeff Z_x$, nor the gradient has to be stored. Let $M_\rho\in\R^{m\times m}$
be the diagonal \textbf{row mask} with entry $0$ for the rows of free surpluses and $1$
otherwise, so that $u^\top M_\rho v$ sums only over the rows of $\Aeff$. With
$u_j = a_j - a_{\rep(j)}$ from~\eqref{eq:AZ-columns}, the entries of the system~\eqref{eq:Heff-Aeff}
are
\begin{equation} \label{eq:implicit}
(\Heff)_{kj} = u_k^\top M_\rho\,u_j,\qquad
\bigl(Z_x^\top(c - \Aeff^\top\pieff)\bigr)_j = \bigl(c_j - a_j^\top M_\rho\pi\bigr)
- \bigl(c_{\rep(j)} - a_{\rep(j)}^\top M_\rho\pi\bigr).
\end{equation}
Both are computed from the sparse columns $a_j$ and the $m$-vector $\pi$. The cost of one entry
of the right-hand side is proportional to the number of nonzeros of two columns, independent
of the total number of columns $n$.


\section{The Lagrange Multipliers}
\label{sec:multipliers}

The multipliers follow from the first block row of the KKT system~\eqref{eq:kkt-system}:
$\frac{1}{\delta}\Abar^\top\Abar p - D_\Wset^\top[\mu;\nu_x;\nu_s] = -g$, i.e.
\begin{equation} \label{eq:multiplier-system}
D_\Wset^\top\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\ \nu_x\\ \nu_s\end{bmatrix} = h,\qquad
h := \frac{1}{\delta}\Abar^\top\Abar\,p + g .
\end{equation}
Once $p$ is known, $h$ is known, and~\eqref{eq:multiplier-system} is an overdetermined but
consistent linear system for the multipliers. The reordering makes its solution explicit.

\begin{theorem}[Lagrange multipliers] \label{thm:multipliers}
With the extended ordering of \cref{thm:reordering} and $h$ split into the blocks
$h = [h_\Sset; h_{\Wx}; h_{\Nx}; h_{\Ws}; h_{\Ns}]$, the solution of~\eqref{eq:multiplier-system}
is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:multipliers}
\mu = h_\Sset,\qquad \nu_x = h_{\Wx} - C_{\Wx}^\top\mu,\qquad \nu_s = h_{\Ws},
\end{equation}
and moreover $h_{\Nx} = C_{\Nx}^\top\mu$ and $h_{\Ns} = 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Multiplying~\eqref{eq:multiplier-system} by $Q^\top$ reorders its rows into the five groups;
$Q^\top D_\Wset^\top = (D_\Wset Q)^\top$ is the transpose of~\eqref{eq:reordered}:
\[
\begin{bmatrix}
I_K & 0 & 0\\
C_{\Wx}^\top & I & 0\\
C_{\Nx}^\top & 0 & 0\\
0 & 0 & I\\
0 & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}
\begin{bmatrix}\mu\\ \nu_x\\ \nu_s\end{bmatrix}
=
\begin{bmatrix} h_\Sset\\ h_{\Wx}\\ h_{\Nx}\\ h_{\Ws}\\ h_{\Ns}\end{bmatrix}.
\]
Read row group by row group: the first gives $\mu = h_\Sset$, the second
$C_{\Wx}^\top\mu + \nu_x = h_{\Wx}$, the fourth $\nu_s = h_{\Ws}$, which is~\eqref{eq:multipliers}.
The third and fifth groups contain no unknown other than $\mu$ and give the remaining
identities.
\end{proof}

The multipliers are only needed at a subspace minimizer, where $p = 0$ and therefore $h = g$.
In this case \cref{thm:multipliers} takes a particularly simple form.

\begin{cor}[Multipliers at a subspace minimizer] \label{cor:multipliers-minimizer}
At a subspace minimizer, with $\pi = \pi(y)$,
\begin{align*}
\mu_k &= c_{S_k} - a_{S_k}^\top\pi && (k = 1,\dots,K),\\
(\nu_x)_j &= c_j - a_j^\top\pi - \mu_{\psi(j)} = \bar c_j(\pi,\mu) && (j\in\Wx),\\
(\nu_s)_i &= \pi_i && (i\in\Ws),
\end{align*}
and $\bar c_j(\pi,\mu) = 0$ for $j\in\Sset\cup\Nx$, $\pi_i = 0$ for $i\in\Ns$.
\end{cor}

\begin{proof}
With $h = g$, the entries of $h$ are $g_j = c_j - a_j^\top\pi$ for column $j$ and $\pi_i$ for
surplus $i$ (by~\eqref{eq:gradient-split}). Column $j$ of $C$ is $e_{\psi(j)}$, so
$(C_{\Wx}^\top\mu)_j = \mu_{\psi(j)}$. Inserting this into \cref{thm:multipliers}:
$\mu_k = g_{S_k}$, $(\nu_x)_j = g_j - \mu_{\psi(j)}$, $(\nu_s)_i = \pi_i$; the identity
$h_{\Nx} = C_{\Nx}^\top\mu$ reads $g_j = \mu_{\psi(j)}$, i.e.\ $\bar c_j = 0$ for $j\in\Nx$; and
$h_{\Ns} = 0$ reads $\pi_i = 0$ for $i\in\Ns$. For a representative, $\bar c_{S_k} = g_{S_k} - \mu_k = 0$.
\end{proof}

\Cref{cor:multipliers-minimizer} gives a concrete form of \cref{rem:same-test}: the optimality
test of the active-set method checks the reduced costs of the working-set columns and the
signs of the duals of the inequality rows with active surpluses. All of them are computed
from the single vector $\pi$. The test touches every column of $\Wx$ once and costs
$O(\nnz(A))$; it is the only operation of the method whose cost grows with the number of
generated columns.

\begin{remark}[``$\le$'' rows] \label{rem:kappa-summary}
For a ``$\le$'' row with slack coefficient $\kappa_i = +1$ (\cref{rem:kappa}), the formulas
above change as follows; everything else, in particular $\Heff$ and $\Aeff$, is unchanged
because $\kappa_i^2 = 1$:
\[
\pi_I = \pihat_I + \tfrac{1}{\delta}\bigl(b_I - A_Ix - D_\kappa s\bigr),\qquad
g_{n+i} = -\kappa_i\pi_i,\qquad
(\nu_s)_i = -\kappa_i\pi_i,\qquad
(p_s)_i = \kappa_i\bigl(\delta\pi_i - (A_Ip_x)_i\bigr).
\]
For $\kappa_i = -1$ these are the formulas of the text.
\end{remark}


\section{Summary of One Iteration}
\label{sec:iteration-summary}

\Cref{alg:nullspace-iteration} collects the computations of one iteration of the active-set
method in nullspace form, and \cref{tab:costs} lists their costs. Here $\bar\ell$ denotes the
average number of nonzeros of a column $a_j$.

\begin{algobox}{One Iteration of the Active-Set Method in Nullspace Form}{alg:nullspace-iteration}
\State \textbf{Given:} iterate $y = [x;s]$, the extended ordering, $\pi = \pi(y)$, the factor $\Heff = LDL^\top$
\State Compute $\mu^{\rho}_k = c_{S_k} - a_{S_k}^\top M_\rho\pi$ and the right-hand side
       $r_j = -\delta\bigl(c_j - a_j^\top M_\rho\pi - \mu^{\rho}_{\psi(j)}\bigr)$, $j\in\Nx$
       \Comment{\eqref{eq:implicit}}
\State Solve $LDL^\top\eta_x = r$
\State Expand $p_x = Z_x\eta_x$ and compute $(p_s)_i = (A_Ip_x)_i - \delta\pi_i$, $i\in\Ns$
       \Comment{\eqref{eq:direction-components}}
\If{$p\approx0$}
    \State $\mu_k = c_{S_k} - a_{S_k}^\top\pi$; \;$\nu_x = $ reduced costs on $\Wx$; \;$\nu_s = \pi$ on $\Ws$
    \State Stop if all multipliers are $\ge-\varepsilon$; otherwise reduce the working set
           (\cref{sec:reducing})
\Else
    \State Ratio test over $\Sset\cup\Nx\cup\Ns$; \;$y\gets y+\alpha p$; \;$\pi\gets\pi - \frac{\alpha}{\delta}\Abar p$
    \State If blocked, expand the working set (\cref{sec:expanding})
\EndIf
\end{algobox}
