\chapter{Efficient Updates of the KKT System}
\label{chap:updates}

\vspace*{\fill}

Every change of the working set in the active-set method adds or removes exactly one index:
a blocking variable is added, or a variable with a negative multiplier is released. The
previous chapter showed that everything the method needs is determined by the extended
ordering and by a factorization of the effective reduced Hessian $\Heff$. This chapter shows
that a change of the working set changes $\Heff$ only by a \textbf{low-rank modification}, so
that its $LDL^\top$ factorization can be updated in place instead of being recomputed.

\medskip

We first introduce a \textbf{fixed-length representation}, in which the factorization has a
fixed dimension and inactive indices are represented by identity rows. We then derive the
updates for the three ways of \textbf{expanding} the working set and the two ways of
\textbf{reducing} it, and translate them into row additions, row deletions and rank-one
modifications of the factorization, which we realize with a sparse direct solver. Its row
deletions leave structural zeros in the factor; we analyse this effect and the
\textbf{periodic refactorization} that controls it. \Cref{chap:dense} develops a dense
alternative that avoids it.

\medskip

Finally, we show that the state of the method can be carried over from one column generation
iteration to the next. A new column and a new stability center leave the factorization
unchanged, which gives a \textbf{hot start} of every master problem. We also analyse the
center-only iterations of \cref{sec:center-update} in this light. The chapter ends with
\textbf{range rows}, whose slacks have an upper bound: reaching it is turned into an ordinary
expansion by a change of the form of the row.

\section{Fixed-Length Representation}
\label{sec:fixed-length}

The number of nullspace columns $|\Nx|$ changes in almost every iteration, and the number of
columns $n$ grows during column generation. Resizing a sparse factorization at every change
would be expensive and complicated. Instead, the factorized matrix is given a fixed dimension.

\begin{definition}[Fixed-length representation] \label{def:fixed-length}
Let $\nmax\ge n$ be a fixed \textbf{column capacity}, the maximal number of columns that column
generation may generate. The \textbf{embedded effective reduced Hessian} is the
$\nmax\times\nmax$ matrix
\[
\bigl(\Heff^{\mathrm{emb}}\bigr)_{jj'} =
\begin{cases}
(\Heff)_{jj'} = u_j^\top \Meff u_{j'}, & j,j'\in\Nx,\\
1, & j = j'\notin\Nx,\\
0, & \text{otherwise,}
\end{cases}
\]
with $u_j = a_j - a_{\rep(j)}$ and the row mask $\Meff$ of~\eqref{eq:implicit}. Index $j$ of
the embedded matrix is column $j$ of the master problem. Indices of columns that are not in
$\Nx$, including columns that have not been generated yet, carry an identity row and column.
\end{definition}

In this representation, a column entering $\Nx$ replaces an identity row by a row of $\Heff$,
and a column leaving $\Nx$ is replaced by an identity row (\cref{fig:fixed-length}). The
dimension never changes. The surpluses do not appear at all: by \cref{thm:decoupling} they
have no index in the factorized matrix, so the inequality rows do not increase the size of
the factorization.

The identity rows do not interfere with the computation of the search direction:

\begin{lemma}[Decoupling of inactive indices] \label{lem:embedding}
Let $\Heff^{\mathrm{emb}} = LDL^\top$ be the $LDL^\top$ factorization without pivoting. Then
for every $j\notin\Nx$, row and column $j$ of $L$ are the unit vectors $e_j^\top$ and $e_j$,
and $D_{jj} = 1$. Consequently, for a right-hand side $\zeta$ that vanishes outside $\Nx$, the
solution of $\Heff^{\mathrm{emb}}\eta = \zeta$ vanishes outside $\Nx$, and its restriction to $\Nx$
solves $\Heff\eta_x = \zeta_{\Nx}$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
A symmetric permutation $P$ that moves the indices of $\Nx$ to the front, keeping their
relative order, turns $\Heff^{\mathrm{emb}}$ into $\diag(\Heff, I)$, because an inactive index has a $1$ on the
diagonal and zeros elsewhere in its row and column. With $\Heff = L_1D_1L_1^\top$, this
block-diagonal matrix has the block-diagonal factorization
\[
\diag(\Heff, I) = \diag(L_1,I)\,\diag(D_1,I)\,\diag(L_1,I)^\top,
\]
which exists because $\Heff$ is positive definite. Since $P$ keeps the relative order of the
indices of $\Nx$ (and of the inactive indices), the permuted factor $P\diag(L_1,I)P^\top$ is again
unit lower triangular, and by the uniqueness of the $LDL^\top$ factorization without pivoting
it is the factor $L$ of $\Heff^{\mathrm{emb}}$. In it, every inactive index $j$ has row and column
$e_j$, and $D_{jj} = 1$. For the second
statement, $\diag(\Heff, I)\,[\eta_x;\eta_0] = [\zeta_{\Nx}; 0]$ gives $\Heff\eta_x = \zeta_{\Nx}$ and
$\eta_0 = 0$.
\end{proof}


\section{Expanding the Working Set}
\label{sec:expanding}

A step is blocked when a free variable reaches zero. The blocking index $q$ is then added to
the working set. There are three cases, depending on the set that $q$ belongs to. For a block
$k$ we write
\[
\mathcal{N}_x^{(k)} = \{\, j\in\Nx : \psi(j) = k \,\}
\]
for the nullspace columns of block $k$; these are the columns whose nullspace vectors
$z_j = e_j - e_{S_k}$ refer to the representative $S_k$.

\begin{lemma}[Ordering after an expansion] \label{lem:expand-ordering}
Let $q$ be the blocking index. The extended ordering is updated as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \label{lem:expand-ordering:i} $q\in\Nx$: $\;\Nx\gets\Nx\setminus\{q\}$, $\;\Wx\gets\Wx\cup\{q\}$.
    \item \label{lem:expand-ordering:ii} $q = S_k\in\Sset$: choose a \textbf{replacement} $S_k'\in\mathcal{N}_x^{(k)}$, the new
          representative of block $k$; then $\Nx\gets\Nx\setminus\{S_k'\}$,
          $\;\Wx\gets\Wx\cup\{q\}$, and $S_k'$ takes the place of $q$ in the list $\Sset$.
    \item \label{lem:expand-ordering:iii} $q$ is the free surplus $i\in\Ns$: $\;\Ns\gets\Ns\setminus\{i\}$, $\;\Ws\gets\Ws\cup\{i\}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

In case~\ref{lem:expand-ordering:ii} the representative itself reaches zero, and another free column of its block
has to take its place in the list $\Sset$. The next lemma shows that one is always
available.

\begin{lemma}[Existence of a replacement] \label{lem:replacement}
In case~\ref{lem:expand-ordering:ii}, the set $\mathcal{N}_x^{(k)}$ is not empty.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By~\eqref{eq:direction-components}, the direction changes the representative by
$p_{S_k} = -\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_x^{(k)}}\eta_j$. The representative can only reach zero if it
decreases, $p_{S_k}<0$, so the sum is not empty.
\end{proof}

The same argument applies to the zero-curvature directions of \cref{chap:singular}, whose
representative components are $\mp\sum_{j\in\mathcal{N}_x^{(k)}}\beta_j$ (upper sign for a released
surplus, lower sign for a released column), except in the block of a
released column $r$, where $d_{S_k}$ contains the additional term $-1$; that case is handled by
\cref{rem:rep-blocks}.

Any $S_k'\in\mathcal{N}_x^{(k)}$ is a valid replacement. A good choice is the column with the largest
weight, $S_k' = \arg\max_{j\in\mathcal{N}_x^{(k)}}x_j$, since it is the least likely to block in the
next step.

\begin{theorem}[Update of the nullspace basis] \label{thm:Z-update}
After an expansion, the nullspace basis changes as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \label{thm:Z-update:i} $q\in\Nx$: the vector $z_q$ is removed.
    \item \label{thm:Z-update:ii} $q = S_k$ with replacement $S_k'$: the vector $z_{S_k'}$ is removed, and for every
          $j\in\mathcal{N}_x^{(k)}\setminus\{S_k'\}$ the vector $z_j$ is replaced by
          \[
          z_j' = e_j - e_{S_k'} = z_j - z_{S_k'} .
          \]
          All other vectors are unchanged.
    \item \label{thm:Z-update:iii} $q$ the surplus $i$: the vector $e_{n+i}$ is removed.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{cor:z-explicit} there is one basis vector per index of $\Nx$ and of $\Ns$, given by
$z_j = e_j - e_{\rep(j)}$ and $z_{n+i} = e_{n+i}$. In cases~\ref{thm:Z-update:i} and~\ref{thm:Z-update:iii} one index leaves $\Nx$ or
$\Ns$, and the representatives do not change, so only its vector disappears. In case~\ref{thm:Z-update:ii},
$S_k'$ leaves $\Nx$ and the representative of block $k$ changes from $q$ to $S_k'$. For
$j\in\mathcal{N}_x^{(k)}\setminus\{S_k'\}$ the new vector is
$z_j' = e_j - e_{S_k'} = (e_j - e_q) - (e_{S_k'} - e_q) = z_j - z_{S_k'}$. The vectors of other blocks refer
to other representatives and do not change.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Update of the effective reduced Hessian, expansion] \label{thm:H-expand}
After an expansion, $\Heff$ changes as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \label{thm:H-expand:i} $q\in\Nx$: row and column $q$ are removed.
    \item \label{thm:H-expand:ii} $q = S_k$ with replacement $S_k'$: row and column $S_k'$ are removed, and for
          $j,j'\in\mathcal{N}_x^{(k)}\setminus\{S_k'\}$ and $\ell\in\Nx\setminus\mathcal{N}_x^{(k)}$
          \[
          (\Heff')_{\ell j} = (\Heff)_{\ell j} - (\Heff)_{\ell S_k'},\qquad
          (\Heff')_{j'j} = (\Heff)_{j'j} - (\Heff)_{j'S_k'} - (\Heff)_{S_k'j} + (\Heff)_{S_k'S_k'};
          \]
          all other entries are unchanged.
    \item \label{thm:H-expand:iii} $q$ the surplus of inequality row $i$: row $i$ of $A_I$ joins $\Aeff$, and
          \begin{equation} \label{eq:rank-one-up}
          \Heff' = \Heff + v_iv_i^\top,\qquad
          v_i = Z_x^\top (A_I)_{i,:}^\top,\qquad (v_i)_j = (A_I)_{ij} - (A_I)_{i,\rep(j)}\;\;(j\in\Nx).
          \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We use $(\Heff)_{\ell j} = (\Aeff z_\ell)^\top(\Aeff z_j)$ from \cref{cor:Aeff}.

(i) The vector $z_q$ disappears, so the column $\Aeff z_q$ of $\Aeff Z_x$ is removed, and with
it row and column $q$ of the Gram matrix $\Heff$.

(ii) The vector $z_{S_k'}$ disappears, which removes row and column $S_k'$. For the remaining columns
of block $k$, $\Aeff z_j' = \Aeff z_j - \Aeff z_{S_k'}$ by \cref{thm:Z-update}. For
$\ell\notin\mathcal{N}_x^{(k)}$ the vector $z_\ell$ is unchanged, so
$(\Heff')_{\ell j} = (\Aeff z_\ell)^\top(\Aeff z_j - \Aeff z_{S_k'}) = (\Heff)_{\ell j} - (\Heff)_{\ell S_k'}$.
For two columns $j, j'$ of block $k$, expanding the product
$(\Aeff z_{j'} - \Aeff z_{S_k'})^\top(\Aeff z_j - \Aeff z_{S_k'})$ gives the second formula.

(iii) The basis $Z_x$ does not change, but $\Aeff$ gains the row $(A_I)_{i,:}$. Hence
\[
\Aeff' Z_x = \begin{bmatrix}\Aeff Z_x\\ (A_I)_{i,:}Z_x\end{bmatrix},\qquad
\Heff' = (\Aeff Z_x)^\top(\Aeff Z_x) + \bigl((A_I)_{i,:}Z_x\bigr)^\top\bigl((A_I)_{i,:}Z_x\bigr)
= \Heff + v_iv_i^\top .
\]
Entry $j$ of $v_i = Z_x^\top(A_I)_{i,:}^\top$ is the product of column $j$ of $Z_x$, which is
$e_j - e_{\rep(j)}$, with row $i$ of $A_I$, i.e.\ $(v_i)_j = (A_I)_{ij} - (A_I)_{i,\rep(j)}$.
\end{proof}

The vector $v_i$ is row $i$ of $B_I = A_IZ_x$. Its entry $(v_i)_j = (A_I)_{ij} - (A_I)_{i,\rep(j)}$ is nonzero only if
the columns $j$ and $\rep(j)$ have different coefficients in row $i$; for the 0/1 data of crew
scheduling, if exactly one of the two schedules covers flight $i$.

\begin{prop} \label{prop:expand-pd}
If $\Heff$ is positive definite before an expansion, it is positive definite after it.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{cor:dimension}, $\Heff$ is positive definite if and only if the vectors $\Aeff z_j$,
$j\in\Nx$, are linearly independent.
(i) Removing one vector from a linearly independent set leaves it independent.
(ii) The new set consists of the unchanged vectors $\Aeff z_\ell$,
$\ell\notin\mathcal{N}_x^{(k)}$, and the vectors $\Aeff z_j - \Aeff z_{S_k'}$ for
$j\in\mathcal{N}_x^{(k)}\setminus\{S_k'\}$. Together with $\Aeff z_{S_k'}$ these $|\Nx|$ vectors span the same
space as the $|\Nx|$ old, independent vectors, so they are independent as well, and so is the
new set without $\Aeff z_{S_k'}$.
(iii) For every $\eta_x\ne0$, $\eta_x^\top(\Heff + v_iv_i^\top)\eta_x = \eta_x^\top\Heff\eta_x +
(v_i^\top\eta_x)^2 > 0$.
\end{proof}


\section{Reducing the Working Set}
\label{sec:reducing}

At a subspace minimizer with a negative multiplier, the index with the most negative
multiplier is released from the working set. By \cref{cor:multipliers-minimizer}, the
multipliers of columns (reduced costs) and of surpluses (duals) are both measured in units
of cost, so they can be compared directly. Releasing an index adds a basis vector to $Z$:

\begin{theorem}[Update of the effective reduced Hessian, reduction] \label{thm:H-reduce}
The two kinds of reduction change $\Heff$ as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item[(iv)] \partlabel{(iv)}{thm:H-reduce:iv}Column $r\in\Wx$ is released ($\Wx\gets\Wx\setminus\{r\}$, $\Nx\gets\Nx\cup\{r\}$).
          The basis gains $z_r = e_r - e_{\rep(r)}$, and
          \begin{equation} \label{eq:bordered}
          \Heff' = \begin{bmatrix}\Heff & \gamma_r\\ \gamma_r^\top & \gamma_{rr}\end{bmatrix},\qquad
          \gamma_r = (\Aeff Z_x)^\top\Aeff z_r,\qquad \gamma_{rr} = \lVert\Aeff z_r\rVert^2 .
          \end{equation}
    \item[(v)] \partlabel{(v)}{thm:H-reduce:v}The surplus of inequality row $i$ is released ($\Ws\gets\Ws\setminus\{i\}$,
          $\Ns\gets\Ns\cup\{i\}$). The basis gains $e_{n+i}$, row $i$ leaves $\Aeff$, and
          \begin{equation} \label{eq:rank-one-down}
          \Heff' = \Heff - v_iv_i^\top
          \end{equation}
          with $v_i = Z_x^\top(A_I)_{i,:}^\top$ as in~\eqref{eq:rank-one-up}.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(iv) The new column part of the basis is $Z_x' = [\,Z_x\;\;z_r\,]$, so
$\Aeff Z_x' = [\,\Aeff Z_x\;\;\Aeff z_r\,]$ and
\[
\Heff' = \begin{bmatrix}(\Aeff Z_x)^\top\\ (\Aeff z_r)^\top\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}\Aeff Z_x & \Aeff z_r\end{bmatrix}
= \begin{bmatrix}\Heff & (\Aeff Z_x)^\top\Aeff z_r\\ (\Aeff z_r)^\top\Aeff Z_x & \lVert\Aeff z_r\rVert^2\end{bmatrix}.
\]
(v) $Z_x$ does not change, and $\Aeff$ loses the row $(A_I)_{i,:}$. This is the reverse of
case~\ref{thm:H-expand:iii} of \cref{thm:H-expand}, so $\Heff = \Heff' + v_iv_i^\top$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Descent after a reduction] \label{lem:descent}
Suppose the reduced Hessian is positive definite after the reduction. Then the next search
direction moves the released variable away from its bound: $p_r>0$ in case~\ref{thm:H-reduce:iv}, and
$(p_s)_i>0$ in case~\ref{thm:H-reduce:v}.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Before the reduction the iterate is a subspace minimizer, so every entry of the reduced
gradient $Z^\top g$ is zero. The reduction adds one basis vector $z$ ($z_r$ or $e_{n+i}$) and
leaves the others unchanged, so the new reduced gradient has the single nonzero entry
$z^\top g$. For $z = z_r$ it is $g_r - g_{\rep(r)}$, and for $z = e_{n+i}$ it is $\pi_i$; by
\cref{cor:multipliers-minimizer} both equal the multiplier $\nu<0$ of the released index. Let
$\ell$ be the position of the new basis vector, so that $Z'^\top g = \nu\,e_\ell$. The new reduced
system $\Hbar'\eta = -\delta Z'^\top g = -\delta\nu\,e_\ell$ gives $\eta = -\delta\nu\,\Hbar'^{-1}e_\ell$,
whose own component is
\[
\eta_\ell = -\delta\nu\,(\Hbar'^{-1})_{\ell\ell} > 0,
\]
since $\Hbar'^{-1}$ is positive definite and $\nu<0$. By~\eqref{eq:direction-components} this
component is the change of the released variable ($p_r = \eta_r$, $(p_s)_i = (\eta_s)_i$).
\end{proof}

Unlike an expansion, a reduction can make the reduced Hessian singular: the new vector $\Aeff z_r$ in
case~\ref{thm:H-reduce:iv} can lie in the span of the old ones, and the downdate in case~\ref{thm:H-reduce:v} can remove the
last direction of curvature. This is the subject of \cref{chap:singular}.


\section{Updating the \texorpdfstring{$LDL^\top$}{LDL\textasciicircum T} Factorization}
\label{sec:ldl-updates}

The updates of \cref{sec:expanding,sec:reducing} are applied to the factorization
$\Heff^{\mathrm{emb}} = LDL^\top$, with $L$ unit lower triangular and $D$ diagonal, in the
natural order of the indices. In this chapter we use the sparse direct solver
CHOLMOD~\cite{chen2008cholmod}, which provides the three required modifications
\cite{davis1999modifying, davis2005row}; \cref{chap:dense} provides the same operations for a
dense factor of $\Heff$ alone:

\subsection{Row additions and deletions}

The following proposition shows what a row addition computes. It explains the cost of the
operation and the meaning of the new pivot, which is used to detect singularity in
\cref{chap:singular}.

\begin{prop}[Row addition] \label{prop:rowadd}
Let row and column $j$ of $\Heff^{\mathrm{emb}}$ currently be $e_j$, and partition the indices
into the groups $1 = \{1,\dots,j-1\}$, $2 = \{j\}$ and $3 = \{j+1,\dots,\nmax\}$:
\[
L = \begin{bmatrix} L_{11} & & \\ 0 & 1 & \\ L_{31} & 0 & L_{33}\end{bmatrix},\qquad
D = \diag(D_1, 1, D_3).
\]
If row and column $j$ are set to a vector $\gamma = [\gamma_1;\gamma_2;\gamma_3]$, split into the
same groups ($\gamma_2$ is a scalar), the new factor has
\begin{align*}
L_{21}^\top &= D_1^{-1}L_{11}^{-1}\gamma_1 &&\text{(triangular solve with the leading block)},\\
D_{22}' &= \gamma_2 - L_{21}D_1L_{21}^\top &&\text{(new pivot)},\\
L_{32} &= \bigl(\gamma_3 - L_{31}D_1L_{21}^\top\bigr)/D_{22}' &&\text{(new column below $j$)},\\
L_{33}'D_3'L_{33}'^\top &= L_{33}D_3L_{33}^\top - D_{22}'\,L_{32}L_{32}^\top &&\text{(rank-one downdate)},
\end{align*}
while $L_{11}$, $D_1$ and $L_{31}$ are unchanged.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
The new factor has the form
$L' = \left[\begin{smallmatrix} L_{11} & & \\ L_{21} & 1 & \\ L_{31} & L_{32} & L_{33}'\end{smallmatrix}\right]$,
$D' = \diag(D_1, D_{22}', D_3')$. Multiplying out $L'D'L'^\top$ block by block and comparing with
the new matrix:
\begin{itemize}
    \item block $(2,1)$: $L_{21}D_1L_{11}^\top = \gamma_1^\top$, which gives $L_{21}$;
    \item entry $(2,2)$: $L_{21}D_1L_{21}^\top + D_{22}' = \gamma_2$, which gives $D_{22}'$;
    \item block $(3,2)$: $L_{31}D_1L_{21}^\top + L_{32}D_{22}' = \gamma_3$, which gives $L_{32}$;
    \item block $(3,3)$: $L_{31}D_1L_{31}^\top + D_{22}'L_{32}L_{32}^\top + L_{33}'D_3'L_{33}'^\top$ must
          equal the unchanged block $L_{31}D_1L_{31}^\top + L_{33}D_3L_{33}^\top$, which gives the
          downdate.
\end{itemize}
The blocks $(1,1)$ and $(3,1)$ do not involve row $j$ and stay as they are.
\end{proof}

A row deletion is the reverse operation: row and column $j$ are reset to $e_j$, which
requires the rank-one \emph{update} $L_{33}D_3L_{33}^\top + D_{22}L_{32}L_{32}^\top$ of the trailing
block, where $D_{22}$ and $L_{32}$ are the current pivot and column of index $j$. Two consequences of \cref{prop:rowadd} are important.

\begin{itemize}
    \item If $j$ is larger than every active index, the trailing block contains no active
          index, the downdate is trivial, and a row addition costs a single triangular solve
          with the active part, $O(|\Nx|^2)$ for a dense factor. This is used by the
          refactorization in \cref{sec:refactorization}.
    \item The pivots $D_1$ are unchanged, the new pivot is $D_{22}'$, and the downdate changes
          the active pivots in $D_3$. Since the determinant of an $LDL^\top$ factorization is the
          product of its pivots, all these changes together multiply the determinant of the
          active part by the Schur complement $\sigma_r = \gamma_{rr} - \gamma_r^\top\Heff^{-1}\gamma_r$
          of~\eqref{eq:bordered}: $\det\Heff' = \det\Heff\cdot\sigma_r$. If $j = r$ is larger than
          every active index, $D_{22}' = \sigma_r$; in general $D_{22}'\ge\sigma_r$, and a singular
          $\Heff'$ may show up in the pivot of a later index rather than in $D_{22}'$
          (\cref{chap:singular}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Rank-one modifications}

The surplus cases~\ref{thm:H-expand:iii} and~\ref{thm:H-reduce:v} modify the whole matrix by $\pm v_iv_i^\top$. The factorization
$LDL^\top\pm v_iv_i^\top = L'D'L'^\top$ is computed by the classical rank-one update and downdate
algorithms \cite{gill1974methods, davis1999modifying}, which process the columns of $L$ along
the path in the elimination tree that starts at the first nonzero of $v_i$. Their cost is
proportional to the number of entries of $L$ in these columns. An update keeps the matrix
positive definite (\cref{prop:expand-pd}). A downdate can make it singular; by the matrix
determinant lemma (write $\Heff - v_iv_i^\top = \Heff(I - \Heff^{-1}v_iv_i^\top)$; the matrix
$I - ab^\top$ has the eigenvalue $1 - b^\top a$ for the eigenvector $a$ and the eigenvalue $1$ on
the orthogonal complement of $b$),
\[
\det\bigl(\Heff - v_iv_i^\top\bigr) = \det\Heff\cdot\bigl(1 - v_i^\top\Heff^{-1}v_i\bigr),
\]
so this happens exactly when $\omega_i := 1 - v_i^\top\Heff^{-1}v_i$ vanishes.
\Cref{chap:singular} shows that $\omega_i\ge0$ always holds for the downdates of the
active-set method, and treats the case $\omega_i = 0$.

\subsection{Sparse solves}

The right-hand side of~\eqref{eq:eff-system} vanishes outside $\Nx$. By \cref{lem:embedding},
the solve only has to touch the rows of $L$ that can be reached from $\Nx$ in the graph of
$L$, the set $\reach_L(\Nx)$. CHOLMOD supports such solves directly. Their cost is independent
of $\nmax$, which is what makes a large capacity $\nmax$ affordable.

\subsection{Assembly of a new row}

A row addition in case~\ref{thm:H-reduce:iv} of \cref{thm:H-reduce} needs the vector $\gamma_r$ of~\eqref{eq:bordered}.
By~\eqref{eq:implicit}, $(\gamma_r)_j = u_j^\top\Meff u_r$ with $u_r = a_r - a_{\rep(r)}$. In an
implementation, $\Meff u_r$ is scattered into a dense $m$-vector once, with the entries of the
rows of free surpluses set to zero. Then $(\gamma_r)_j = a_j^\top(\Meff u_r) - a_{\rep(j)}^\top(\Meff u_r)$
is computed for every $j\in\Nx$, where the products with the $K$ representatives are computed once and reused. The
cost is $O\bigl((|\Nx| + K)\,\bar\ell\bigr)$, independent of $n$. A representative swap
(case~\ref{lem:expand-ordering:ii} of \cref{lem:expand-ordering}) first deletes all rows of $\mathcal{N}_x^{(k)}$, including the
row of $S_k'$, and then adds the rows of $\mathcal{N}_x^{(k)}\setminus\{S_k'\}$ again, assembled with
the new vectors $z_j'$. Every intermediate matrix is then the Gram matrix of a subset of the old
vectors, or of such a subset together with some of the new vectors $\Aeff z_j'$. Together with
$\Aeff z_{S_k'}$, the old and the new vectors each form a basis of the same space
(\cref{prop:expand-pd}), so every such set is linearly independent, and the matrix stays
positive definite.
This costs the same as applying the formulas of \cref{thm:H-expand}, and it needs no stored copy
of $\Heff$.


\section{Structural Zeros and Periodic Refactorization}
\label{sec:refactorization}

A row deletion sets row and column $j$ of $L$ to $e_j$ \emph{numerically}, but a sparse
factorization keeps the positions of the removed entries in its sparsity pattern: the
pattern of a column of a simplicial factor can only grow. The same holds for rank-one
modifications. Over a long sequence of updates, the stored pattern of $L$ therefore becomes
the union of the patterns of all factors that ever existed. To quantify this effect we
distinguish the following quantities, which are measured in \cref{sec:results-fill}.

\begin{definition}[Stored and effective entries] \label{def:stored-effective}
The \textbf{stored entries} of $L$ are the off-diagonal positions in its sparsity pattern.
The \textbf{effective entries} are the stored entries whose value is nonzero. The difference
are the \textbf{structural zeros}. The \textbf{dense equivalent} is $|\Nx|(|\Nx|-1)/2$, the
number of off-diagonal entries of a dense factor of $\Heff$.
\end{definition}

Every solve and every update iterates over the stored entries of the columns it touches,
including the structural zeros. Moreover, structural zeros can connect active indices to
indices that are no longer active, which enlarges $\reach_L(\Nx)$. The stored pattern only
returns to the effective one when the factorization is rebuilt.

\begin{definition}[Refactorization] \label{def:refactorization}
A \textbf{refactorization} resets the factor to the identity and re-activates all indices of
$\Nx$ by row additions \emph{in increasing index order}, with the rows assembled as in
\cref{sec:ldl-updates}.
\end{definition}

\begin{prop}[Cost of a refactorization] \label{prop:refactor-cost}
A refactorization costs $O(|\Nx|^3)$ arithmetic operations for the factor (about
$|\Nx|^3/3$ for a dense $\Heff$) and $O\bigl(|\Nx|(|\Nx|+K)\,\bar\ell\bigr)$ for the assembly of
the rows. Its stored pattern contains no entries left over from deleted rows.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
When the indices are added in increasing order, every new index is the largest active index,
so by \cref{prop:rowadd} each row addition is a single triangular solve with the current
active part, which costs $O(\ell^2)$ for the $\ell$-th index. Summing over $\ell = 1,\dots,|\Nx|$ gives
$O(|\Nx|^3)$, about $|\Nx|^3/3$ as for a dense Cholesky factorization. Each row costs
$O\bigl((|\Nx|+K)\,\bar\ell\bigr)$ to assemble (\cref{sec:ldl-updates}). Since no row is ever
deleted during a refactorization, every stored entry is computed from the current matrix.
\end{proof}

The method refactorizes after every $\tau$ updates of the factorization (row additions, row
deletions and rank-one modifications), and whenever a pivot has become too small
(\cref{chap:singular}). The \textbf{refactorization interval} $\tau$ balances the cost of a
rebuild against the growth of the structural zeros, which is roughly proportional to the number
of updates. Its effect is studied in \cref{sec:results-fill}.

\begin{remark}[Natural ordering] \label{rem:natural-ordering}
Sparse direct solvers usually reorder a matrix to reduce fill-in. For $\Heff$ this would gain
nothing: the effective factor is almost exactly as dense as a dense factor of $\Heff$
(\cref{sec:results-fill}), because columns of the same block share their representative and
are therefore strongly coupled. The natural ordering has two advantages: index $j$ of the
factor is column $j$ of the master problem, and the refactorization of
\cref{def:refactorization} only appends rows. The density of the factor is also the starting
point of \cref{chap:dense}.
\end{remark}


\section{Hot Start Between Column Generation Iterations}
\label{sec:hot-start}

Between two master problems, regularized column generation adds columns and moves the
stability center (\cref{alg:rcg}). Both changes can be absorbed without touching the
factorization.

\begin{theorem}[Hot start] \label{thm:hot-start}
Let $y = [x;s]$ be the final iterate of a master problem, with its extended ordering and the
factorization of $\Heff$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \label{thm:hot-start:i} \textbf{New column.} If a column $n+1\le\nmax$ is added with weight $x_{n+1} = 0$
          and placed in the working set $\Wx$, then $y$ remains feasible, $\pi(y)$ is
          unchanged, and the nullspace basis, $\Aeff$, $\Heff$ and its factorization are
          unchanged. The multiplier of the new column is its reduced cost
          $c_{n+1} - a_{n+1}^\top\pi - \mu_{\psi(n+1)}$.
    \item \label{thm:hot-start:ii} \textbf{New center.} If the center changes from $\pihat$ to $\pihat + \Delta$,
          then $y$ remains feasible, the ordering and the factorization are unchanged, and the
          dual estimate becomes $\pi(y) + \Delta$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) The new column has weight zero, so the convexity constraint of its block and the product
$\Abar y$ are unchanged; hence $y$ stays feasible and $\pi(y) = \pihat + \frac{1}{\delta}(b - \Abar y)$
is unchanged. The new index is placed in $\Wx$, so the sets $\Sset$, $\Nx$, $\Ns$, and with them
$Z$, $\Aeff$ and $\Heff$, are unchanged. In the fixed-length representation, index $n+1$
already carries an identity row, so the factorization is unchanged too. By
\cref{cor:multipliers-minimizer}, the multiplier of a working-set column is its reduced cost
$c_{n+1} - a_{n+1}^\top\pi - \mu_{\psi(n+1)}$.
(ii) The constraints $\Cbar y = \one$, $y\ge0$ do not involve $\pihat$, and
$\Heff = (\Aeff Z_x)^\top(\Aeff Z_x)$ does not depend on it either. Finally
$\pi(y) = (\pihat + \Delta) + \frac{1}{\delta}(b - \Abar y)$ is the old dual estimate plus $\Delta$.
\end{proof}

If only a column is added, the previous optimum remains a subspace minimizer, and its only
negative multiplier is that of the new column: pricing found its reduced cost below
$-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rc}}$ (with $\pi^+ = \pi$ up to the master tolerance). The next master
problem then starts by releasing the new column (case~\ref{thm:H-reduce:iv} of \cref{thm:H-reduce}). Under the
proximal point rule the center moves at the same time; the gradient changes, the iterate is in
general no longer a subspace minimizer, and the next master problem starts with a direction
computation on the inherited factorization. The surpluses are never added or
removed, and their status is carried over like that of the columns.

\subsection{Center-only iterations}

The hot start also explains the behaviour of the center-only iterations of
\cref{sec:center-update}, in which the center moves to the current dual and no column is
added. Let $\Pi = \Abar Z\,\Hbar^{-1}(\Abar Z)^\top$ denote the orthogonal projector onto the range
of $\Abar Z$.

\begin{prop}[Center-only iterations] \label{prop:center-only}
Let $y$ be optimal for the current master problem, with scaled residual
$w = (b - \Abar y)/\delta$ (the auxiliary variable of~\eqref{eq:explicit}) and dual
$\pi = \pihat + w$, and let the center move to $\pihat' = \pi$. Suppose that the next
master problem takes one full step and keeps the working set. Then
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \label{prop:center-only:i} the new scaled residual is $w' = (I-\Pi)\,w$ and the new dual is $\pi' = \pi + (I-\Pi)\,w$;
    \item \label{prop:center-only:ii} if the working set stays the same in the following center-only iterations, the
          primal iterate no longer changes, and every further iteration moves the dual by the
          fixed vector $w'$:
          \[
          \pi^{(t+1)} = \pi^{(t)} + w',\qquad
          (\nu_x)_j^{(t+1)} = (\nu_x)_j^{(t)} - u_j^\top w',\qquad
          (\nu_s)_i^{(t+1)} = (\nu_s)_i^{(t)} + (w'_I)_i,
          \]
          where $t$ counts the center-only iterations and $u_j = a_j - a_{\rep(j)}$ for every
          column $j$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
(i) The center moves by $\Delta = \pihat' - \pihat = \pi - \pihat = w$. By
\cref{thm:hot-start}, the dual at the old iterate becomes $\pi + \Delta$, and since the gradient
is $g = \cbar - \Abar^\top\pi$ (\cref{lem:gradient-dual}), it changes by $-\Abar^\top\Delta$. The
old iterate was optimal, so its reduced gradient was zero, and the new reduced gradient is
\[
Z^\top g' = Z^\top g - Z^\top\Abar^\top\Delta = -(\Abar Z)^\top\Delta .
\]
The reduced system $\Hbar\eta = -\delta Z^\top g'$ gives $\eta = \delta\,\Hbar^{-1}(\Abar Z)^\top\Delta$,
hence $\Abar p = \Abar Z\eta = \delta\,\Pi\Delta$. After the full step, the dual is
\[
\pi' = (\pi + \Delta) - \tfrac{1}{\delta}\Abar p = \pi + \Delta - \Pi\Delta = \pi + (I - \Pi)\Delta,
\]
and by \cref{prop:residual} the new scaled residual is $w' = \pi' - \pihat' = (I-\Pi)\Delta = (I-\Pi)\,w$.

(ii) The vector $w' = (I-\Pi)w$ is orthogonal to the range of $\Abar Z$, i.e.\
$(\Abar Z)^\top w' = 0$. In the next center-only iteration the center moves by
$\Delta' = w'$, and the same computation as in~\ref{prop:center-only:i} gives the reduced gradient
$-(\Abar Z)^\top\Delta' = 0$: the direction is zero and $y$ does not move. The dual becomes
$\pi' + \Delta'$; since the new center is $\pi'$, the scaled residual is
$(\pi'+\Delta') - \pi' = w'$ again, and the argument repeats. The multipliers follow from \cref{cor:multipliers-minimizer}. A shift of $\pi$ by
$\Delta'$ changes $g_j$ by $-a_j^\top\Delta'$, hence $(\nu_x)_j = g_j - g_{\rep(j)}$ by
$-u_j^\top\Delta'$; and $(\nu_s)_i = \pi_i$ changes by $(\Delta'_I)_i$.
\end{proof}

\Cref{prop:center-only} shows that in a center-only phase the dual walks along a fixed
direction with step length $\lVert w'\rVert$, while the primal iterate stays put. The
walk ends when a multiplier changes sign, so that the working set changes, or when pricing
finds an improving column. If the scaled residual $w'$ is small, many such iterations are needed,
which explains the long tail of center-only iterations observed in \cref{sec:results-phases}.
Each of them is cheap: one direction computation, which returns zero, and one multiplier test.
The step length is proportional to $1/\delta$, and a change of $\delta$ costs nothing by
\cref{rem:factor-independent}; the geometric rule of \cref{sec:adaptive} uses this to remove
the tail (\cref{sec:results-adaptive}).


\section{The Complete Method}
\label{sec:complete}

\Cref{alg:hot-qpas} combines the results of
\cref{chap:qpas,chap:nullspace,chap:updates}. It is called in the first line of the loop of
regularized column generation (\cref{alg:rcg}), and its state, consisting of the iterate, the
extended ordering and the factorization, is kept from one call to the next.

\begin{algobox}{Hot-Started Nullspace Active-Set Method}{alg:hot-qpas}
\State \textbf{Input:} previous state (iterate $y$, ordering, factor), new columns, center $\pihat$
\State Put the new columns into $\Wx$ with weight $0$ \Comment{\cref{thm:hot-start}}
\State $\pi\gets\pihat + (b - \Abar y)/\delta$
\Repeat
    \If{a full step was taken and the working set has not changed since}
        \State $p\gets0$ \Comment{\cref{prop:full-step}}
    \Else
        \State Compute $p$ by \cref{alg:nullspace-iteration} (lines 2--4)
    \EndIf
    \If{$p\approx0$}
        \State Compute the multipliers (\cref{cor:multipliers-minimizer})
        \If{$\nu_x\ge-\varepsilon$ and $\nu_s\ge-\varepsilon$} \textbf{return} $y$, $\pi$, $\mu$ \EndIf
        \State Release the index $\rho$ with the most negative multiplier by \cref{alg:singular}
    \Else
        \State Ratio test over $\Sset\cup\Nx\cup\Ns$, with the upper bounds of free range
               slacks (\cref{prop:range-ratio}); $y\gets y + \alpha p$;
               $\pi\gets\pi - \frac{\alpha}{\delta}\Abar p$
        \If{blocked by $q$}
            \State Expand: \code{rowdel} ($q\in\Nx$), representative swap ($q\in\Sset$), or
                   update ($q\in\Ns$, after a bound flip if $q$ reached its upper bound,
                   \cref{thm:flip-expand})
        \EndIf
    \EndIf
    \State Refactorize if $\tau$ updates have passed since the last refactorization
           \Comment{sparse factor only}, or if an expansion violated the pivot invariant
\Until{returned}
\end{algobox}

The algorithm accesses the factorization only through the operations of
\cref{sec:ldl-updates}: row additions, row deletions, rank-one modifications and solves. It is
therefore independent of how the factor is stored. With the dense factor of
\cref{chap:dense}, the periodic refactorization in the last line is dropped, and only the
pivot test remains (\cref{cor:expand-pivots} and the remark after it).


\section{Range Rows in the Active-Set Method}
\label{sec:range-qpas}

This section extends the method to the range rows of \cref{sec:range}. Let $R$ be the set of
inequality rows that are range rows. The slack of a row $i\in R$ has the bounds
$0\le s_i\le U_i$, and the row is in its lower or its upper form, indicated by $\kappa_i$.
The regularized master problem~\eqref{eq:qp} then has the additional bounds
\begin{equation} \label{eq:range-bounds}
s_i\le U_i,\qquad i\in R,
\end{equation}
and all formulas involving surpluses carry the factor $\kappa_i$ of \cref{rem:kappa-summary}.
We show that the method needs only two additions, a longer ratio test and the bound flip, and
that the flip is an ordinary expansion of the working set.

\begin{theorem}[Optimality conditions with range rows] \label{thm:kkt-range}
A feasible point $y = [x;s]$ of~\eqref{eq:qp} with the bounds~\eqref{eq:range-bounds} is
optimal if and only if there is a $\mu\in\R^K$ such that condition~\ref{thm:kkt-master:i} of
\cref{thm:kkt-master} holds, conditions~\ref{thm:kkt-master:ii}--\ref{thm:kkt-master:iii} hold
in the form $-\kappa_i\pi_i\ge0$, with equality if $s_i>0$, for every one-sided inequality
row, and for every range row $i\in R$
\[
-\kappa_i\pi_i\ge0 \;\text{ if } s_i = 0,\qquad
\pi_i = 0 \;\text{ if } 0<s_i<U_i,\qquad
-\kappa_i\pi_i\le0 \;\text{ if } s_i = U_i .
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The problem is a convex quadratic program with linear constraints, so the KKT conditions are
necessary and sufficient. They are those of \cref{thm:kkt-master} with an additional
multiplier $\omega_i\ge0$ for each upper bound $s_i\le U_i$, with $\omega_i = 0$ if $s_i<U_i$.
The stationarity condition of slack $i\in R$ reads $g_{n+i} = \nu_{n+i} - \omega_i$, where
$g_{n+i} = -\kappa_i\pi_i$ (\cref{rem:kappa-summary}) and $\nu_{n+i}\ge0$ vanishes if $s_i>0$.
For $s_i = 0$ this is $-\kappa_i\pi_i = \nu_{n+i}\ge0$, for $0<s_i<U_i$ it is $-\kappa_i\pi_i = 0$,
and for $s_i = U_i>0$ it is $-\kappa_i\pi_i = -\omega_i\le0$.
\end{proof}

In the lower form ($\kappa_i = -1$) this reads $\pi_i\ge0$ at the lower bound $\ell_i$ of the
row and $\pi_i\le0$ at its upper bound $u_i$, which are the complementarity conditions of the
free dual of a range row in \cref{prop:lagrangian-general}. The condition at $s_i = U_i$ is
the only one that the multiplier test of \cref{alg:hot-qpas} cannot check, since that test only
sees variables at zero. The bound flip removes this case.

\begin{prop}[Ratio test with upper bounds] \label{prop:range-ratio}
Let $y$ be feasible and $p$ a search direction. The step $y + \alpha p$ satisfies the
bounds~\eqref{eq:range-bounds} for all $\alpha\in[0,\alpha_R]$ with
\[
\alpha_R = \min_{\substack{i\in R\cap\Ns\\ (p_s)_i>0}} \frac{U_i - s_i}{(p_s)_i}
\qquad(\min\emptyset = \infty),
\]
and for no larger $\alpha$ if $\alpha_R<\infty$. Slacks in $\Ws$ do not move.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Slacks in $\Ws$ have $(p_s)_i = 0$. A free slack with $(p_s)_i\le0$ does not approach $U_i$.
For $(p_s)_i>0$ the bound $s_i + \alpha(p_s)_i\le U_i$ is equivalent to
$\alpha\le(U_i - s_i)/(p_s)_i$.
\end{proof}

The ratio test of \cref{alg:hot-qpas} therefore takes the minimum of its usual value and
$\alpha_R$. If $\alpha_R$ attains the minimum, the step is \textbf{blocked by the upper bound}
of a range slack $i$, and $s_i = U_i$ after the step.

\begin{theorem}[The bound flip is an expansion] \label{thm:flip-expand}
Let the step be blocked by the upper bound of the free range slack $i$, and apply the bound
flip~\eqref{eq:bound-flip} to row $i$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \label{thm:flip-expand:i} the dual estimate $\pi(y)$, the master objective $\Phi$
          of~\eqref{eq:objective-relation}, the gradient components of all columns, the
          extended ordering, the nullspace basis, $\Aeff$ and $\Heff$ are unchanged, and the
          slack of row $i$ is now $s_i = 0$;
    \item \label{thm:flip-expand:ii} adding this slack to the working set is case~\ref{lem:expand-ordering:iii}
          of \cref{lem:expand-ordering}: the factorization receives the rank-one update
          $\Heff' = \Heff + v_iv_i^\top$ of \cref{thm:H-expand}~\ref{thm:H-expand:iii}, with the same
          vector $v_i$ as for a slack reaching zero;
    \item \label{thm:flip-expand:iii} afterwards every range slack in $\Ws$ is at zero in its current form, and the
          multiplier of slack $i$ in later multiplier tests is $-\kappa_i\pi_i$ with the new
          $\kappa_i$, the sign condition of the bound that the row has reached.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
(i) By \cref{lem:bound-flip}, $b - \Abar y$ is unchanged, and with it $\pi(y) = \pihat + (b - \Abar y)/\delta$
and, by \cref{prop:objective-value}, $\Phi$. The gradient of column $j$ is $c_j - a_j^\top\pi$
(\cref{lem:gradient-dual}) and does not change. The flip changes neither the columns $a_j$ nor
the sets of the ordering, and since slack $i$ is free, row $i$ is not part of $\Aeff$; $Z$,
$\Aeff$ and $\Heff$ depend only on the columns, the ordering and the rows of $\Aeff$
(\cref{cor:Aeff,rem:factor-independent}). The new slack is $U_i - s_i = 0$.
(ii) After the flip, slack $i$ is a free slack at its lower bound, which is the situation of
case~\ref{lem:expand-ordering:iii}. Row $i$ joins $\Aeff$ with its column coefficients
$(A_I)_{i,:}$, which the flip does not change; $\kappa_i$ does not enter $\Heff$
(\cref{rem:kappa-summary}).
(iii) The new slack is at zero, and its multiplier is $g_{n+i} = -\kappa_i\pi_i$ with the
current $\kappa_i$ (\cref{rem:kappa-summary}). For the lower form after the flip this is
$\pi_i\ge0$, for the upper form $\pi_i\le0$, as in \cref{thm:kkt-range}.
\end{proof}

The eliminated objective $f$ of~\eqref{eq:eliminated} changes by a constant under the flip,
since it differs from $\Phi$ by $\frac{1}{2\delta}\lVert b\rVert^2 + \pihat^\top b$
(\cref{thm:eliminated}); its gradient component of slack $i$ changes sign together with the
variable. Neither affects the method, which works with $\pi$ and the gradient of the columns.

\begin{cor}[Correctness with range rows] \label{cor:range-correct}
If the ratio test of \cref{alg:hot-qpas} includes $\alpha_R$ and every block by an upper bound
is followed by the bound flip and the expansion of \cref{thm:flip-expand}, then every iterate
satisfies~\eqref{eq:range-bounds}, every range slack in $\Ws$ is at zero in its current form,
and the stopping test of \cref{alg:hot-qpas} certifies the optimality conditions of
\cref{thm:kkt-range}.
\end{cor}

\begin{proof}
Feasibility follows from \cref{prop:range-ratio}, and the statement on $\Ws$ from
\cref{thm:flip-expand}~\ref{thm:flip-expand:iii}, since a release only removes a slack from $\Ws$.
At a subspace minimizer, the free slacks have $\pi_i = 0$ (\cref{prop:free-duals}), which
satisfies the condition of \cref{thm:kkt-range} for every value of $s_i$, including
$s_i = U_i$. The slacks in $\Ws$ are at zero, and their condition $-\kappa_i\pi_i\ge0$ is part
of the multiplier test. The conditions of the columns are unchanged.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Releases, singular steps and the hot start.} A range slack with a negative
multiplier is released as any other slack (\cref{thm:H-reduce}~\ref{thm:H-reduce:v}); the
direction then moves it into the interior of $[0,U_i]$, away from the bound of its current form.
The zero-curvature steps of \cref{chap:singular} use the same extended ratio test. The proof of
\cref{thm:removal} only uses $d_q\ne0$ for the blocking index $q$, so it also holds when $q$ is
blocked by its upper bound, and \cref{prop:stays-minimizer} holds because the flip does not
change $\pi$. The forms $\kappa_i$ and right-hand sides $b_i$ belong to the state of the method
and are kept from one column generation iteration to the next; new columns and new centers do
not change them (\cref{thm:hot-start}).

\begin{cor}[Exact termination with range rows] \label{cor:range-termination}
\Cref{thm:exact-termination} holds for problems with ``$\le$'' rows and range rows, with the
dual~\eqref{eq:master-dual} replaced by the problem of maximizing
$\sum_i\phi_i(\pi_i) + \one^\top\mu$ subject to the reduced cost conditions, with $\phi_i$ as in
\cref{prop:lagrangian-general}.
\end{cor}

\begin{proof}
If $\pi = \pihat$, then $\Abar y = b$ by \cref{prop:residual}, so every row satisfies
$A_{i,:}\lambda + \kappa_is_i = b_i$ with $0\le s_i\le U_i$, i.e.\ $\ell_i\le A_{i,:}\lambda\le u_i$. By
\cref{thm:kkt-range}, $\pi_i\ge0$ if the row is at $\ell_i<u_i$, $\pi_i\le0$ if it is at
$u_i>\ell_i$, and $\pi_i = 0$ if it lies strictly between, so $\phi_i(\pi_i)$ is finite and equals
$\pi_iA_{i,:}\lambda$. Together with the reduced cost conditions, which pricing extends to all
columns, and the duality argument of \cref{prop:lagrangian-general}, $\lambda$ and $(\pi,\mu)$
are feasible and have equal objective values, so both are optimal.
\end{proof}

The cost of a bound flip is $O(1)$ for the flip itself plus the rank-one update of the
expansion. A run with range rows therefore has the same kind of iterations as a run with
one-sided rows, with expansions at either bound of the slacks. \Cref{sec:results-range}
measures this.
