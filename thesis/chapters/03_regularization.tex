\chapter{Regularization of the Master Problem}
\label{chap:regularization}

\vspace*{\fill}

This chapter replaces the linear restricted master problem by a regularized one. The
regularization is formulated in the dual space, where it adds a quadratic penalty on the
distance between the dual iterate and a \textbf{stability center}. Translated back to the
primal space, it yields a convex quadratic program with an auxiliary variable, the
\textbf{explicit regularized master problem}.

\medskip

Because all linking rows of the unified formulation are equalities, the auxiliary variable
can be eliminated for equality and inequality rows alike. The result, the
\textbf{eliminated regularized master problem}, is a convex quadratic program whose only
constraints are the convexity constraints and the nonnegativity of the variables. This is the
problem that the active-set method of the following chapters solves.

\medskip

Two identities derived in this chapter are used throughout the rest of the work. The first
expresses the duals of the linking rows as an explicit function of the primal iterate. The
second shows that the gradient of the quadratic objective consists of the reduced costs of
the columns and the duals of the inequality rows. The chapter then states the regularized
column generation algorithm, its exact stopping criterion and the accuracy that this
criterion guarantees. It ends with a rule that adapts the stabilization parameter during the
run, based on the interpretation of $\delta$ as the step size of a proximal point method.

\vspace*{\fill}

\section{Proximal Stabilization of the Dual}
\label{sec:proximal}

Consider the restricted master problem in the unified form~\eqref{eq:unified}, over the
columns $\mathcal{J}'$ and all $m_I$ surpluses. By \cref{lem:unified}, its dual is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:restricted-dual}
\max_{\pi,\,\mu}\; b^\top\pi + \one^\top\mu
\qquad\text{s.t.}\qquad
c_j - a_j^\top\pi - \mu_{\psi(j)}\ge0 \;\;(j\in\mathcal{J}'),\qquad \pi_I\ge0 .
\end{equation}
The dual solutions returned by a simplex solver are vertices of this feasible region, which
causes the instability described in \cref{rem:instability}. Proximal stabilization adds a penalty on the
distance to a given point $\pihat\in\R^m$.

\begin{definition}[Stabilized dual problem] \label{def:stabilized-dual}
Let $\pihat\in\R^m$ be the \textbf{stability center} and $\delta>0$ the
\textbf{stabilization parameter}. The \textbf{stabilized dual problem} is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:stabilized-dual}
\max_{\pi,\,\mu}\; b^\top\pi + \one^\top\mu - \frac{\delta}{2}\,\lVert\pi-\pihat\rVert^2
\qquad\text{s.t.}\qquad
c_j - a_j^\top\pi - \mu_{\psi(j)}\ge0 \;\;(j\in\mathcal{J}'),\qquad \pi_I\ge0 .
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

The objective of~\eqref{eq:stabilized-dual} is strictly concave in $\pi$, so the optimal
$\pi$ is unique. A large $\delta$ keeps $\pi$ close to $\pihat$ (strong stabilization), and
$\delta\to0$ recovers~\eqref{eq:restricted-dual}. Replacing the center $\pihat$ by the
solution $\pi$ and solving again is one step of the \textbf{proximal point method}
\cite{rockafellar1976monotone} applied to the restricted dual problem. If the center is only
moved when it improves the Lagrangian bound, we obtain a \textbf{proximal bundle method}
\cite{kiwiel1990proximity, briant2008comparison}. Both rules are discussed in
\cref{sec:center-update}.


\section{The Explicit Regularized Master Problem}
\label{sec:explicit}

Column generation needs a primal master problem: its solution provides the weights $\lambda$,
and its duals are passed to the pricing problem. The primal counterpart of the stabilized
dual~\eqref{eq:stabilized-dual} has an auxiliary vector $w\in\R^m$, one entry per linking row.

\begin{definition}[Explicit regularized master problem] \label{def:explicit}
The \textbf{explicit regularized master problem} is
\begin{subequations} \label{eq:explicit}
\begin{align}
\min_{y,\,w}\quad & \cbar^\top y + \frac{\delta}{2}\lVert w + \pihat\rVert^2
                     - \frac{\delta}{2}\lVert\pihat\rVert^2 \\
\text{s.t.}\quad & \Abar y + \delta w = b, && (\pi) \label{eq:explicit-link}\\
& \Cbar y = \one, && (\mu)\\
& y\ge0 .
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem} \label{thm:explicit-duality}
The dual of~\eqref{eq:explicit} is the stabilized dual problem~\eqref{eq:stabilized-dual}.
Strong duality holds, and at a pair of optimal solutions
\begin{equation} \label{eq:pi-w}
\pi = \pihat + w .
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
With multipliers $\pi$ for $\Abar y + \delta w = b$, $\mu$ for $\Cbar y = \one$ and $\nu\ge0$
for $y\ge0$, the Lagrangian of~\eqref{eq:explicit} is
\[
\mathcal{L}(y,w;\pi,\mu,\nu) = \cbar^\top y + \tfrac{\delta}{2}\lVert w+\pihat\rVert^2
- \tfrac{\delta}{2}\lVert\pihat\rVert^2
+ \pi^\top(b - \Abar y - \delta w) + \mu^\top(\one - \Cbar y) - \nu^\top y .
\]
The dual function is the infimum of $\mathcal{L}$ over $y$ and $w$, which we compute separately.

\emph{Infimum over $w$.} $\mathcal{L}$ is a strictly convex quadratic function of $w$ with
gradient $\delta(w+\pihat) - \delta\pi$, so the minimizer is $w = \pi - \pihat$. Inserting
it gives the $w$-dependent terms
\[
\tfrac{\delta}{2}\lVert\pi\rVert^2 - \tfrac{\delta}{2}\lVert\pihat\rVert^2
- \delta\pi^\top(\pi-\pihat) = -\tfrac{\delta}{2}\lVert\pi-\pihat\rVert^2 .
\]

\emph{Infimum over $y$.} $\mathcal{L}$ is linear in $y$ with coefficient vector
$\cbar - \Abar^\top\pi - \Cbar^\top\mu - \nu$. The infimum is finite (and then the term
vanishes) only if this vector is zero, i.e.\ only if $\cbar - \Abar^\top\pi - \Cbar^\top\mu = \nu\ge0$.

Together, the dual problem is to maximize $b^\top\pi + \one^\top\mu - \frac{\delta}{2}\lVert\pi-\pihat\rVert^2$
subject to $\cbar - \Abar^\top\pi - \Cbar^\top\mu\ge0$. As in the proof of \cref{lem:unified}, the
entry of this vector for $\lambda_j$ is $c_j - a_j^\top\pi - \mu_{\psi(j)}$, and the entry for
$s_i$ is $(\pi_I)_i$. This is exactly~\eqref{eq:stabilized-dual}.

Problem~\eqref{eq:explicit} is a convex quadratic program with linear constraints. It is
feasible, since $\mathcal{J}'$ contains a column of every block and $w$ is free, and its
objective is bounded below, since $\cbar^\top y = c^\top\lambda$ with $0\le\lambda\le1$ and
$\frac{\delta}{2}\lVert w+\pihat\rVert^2\ge0$. A convex quadratic program that is feasible and
bounded below attains its minimum (Frank--Wolfe theorem), and with linear constraints the KKT
conditions hold there. This gives strong duality. At an optimal pair, $(y,w)$ minimizes
$\mathcal{L}$ for the optimal multipliers, and the minimizer in $w$ is unique, so
$w = \pi - \pihat$, which is~\eqref{eq:pi-w}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark} \label{rem:sign-in-dual}
\Cref{thm:explicit-duality} shows that the sign condition $\pi_I\ge0$ becomes part of the
stabilized dual problem automatically, through the surplus columns. The stability center
$\pihat$ itself may violate it; the proximal step then moves $\pi$ back into the region
$\pi_I\ge0$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark} \label{rem:why-surplus}
Without surplus variables, an inequality row $(A_I\lambda)_i\ge(b_I)_i$ would enter the
explicit problem as $(A_I\lambda)_i + \delta w_i\ge(b_I)_i$, and $w_i$ would no longer be
determined by $\lambda$. The surpluses make every row of~\eqref{eq:explicit-link} an
equality, which is what allows $w$ to be eliminated for all rows in the next section.
\end{remark}


\section{The Eliminated Regularized Master Problem}
\label{sec:eliminated}

The explicit problem~\eqref{eq:explicit} has $m$ more variables than necessary: every
feasible point satisfies $\Abar y + \delta w = b$, so $w = (b - \Abar y)/\delta$ is determined
by $y$. Substituting this into the objective removes $w$ completely.

\begin{theorem}[Eliminated regularized master problem] \label{thm:eliminated}
Problem~\eqref{eq:explicit} is equivalent to the convex quadratic program
\begin{subequations} \label{eq:eliminated}
\begin{align}
\min_{y}\quad & f(y) = \frac{1}{2\delta}\, y^\top\Abar^\top\Abar\, y + \ctil^\top y \\
\text{s.t.}\quad & \Cbar y = \one, \label{eq:eliminated-conv}\\
& y\ge0, \label{eq:eliminated-bounds}
\end{align}
\end{subequations}
with the linear cost
\begin{equation} \label{eq:ctilde}
\ctil = \cbar - \Abar^\top\Bigl(\pihat + \frac{b}{\delta}\Bigr).
\end{equation}
More precisely, $(y, w)$ is optimal for~\eqref{eq:explicit} if and only if $y$ is optimal
for~\eqref{eq:eliminated} and $w = (b - \Abar y)/\delta$. The objective values are related by
\begin{equation} \label{eq:objective-relation}
\Phi(y) := \cbar^\top y + \frac{\delta}{2}\lVert w+\pihat\rVert^2 - \frac{\delta}{2}\lVert\pihat\rVert^2
= f(y) + \frac{1}{2\delta}\lVert b\rVert^2 + \pihat^\top b,
\end{equation}
where $w = (b - \Abar y)/\delta$; this defines the master objective $\Phi$ for every $y$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Expanding the squares,
\[
\tfrac{\delta}{2}\lVert w + \pihat\rVert^2 - \tfrac{\delta}{2}\lVert\pihat\rVert^2
= \tfrac{\delta}{2}\lVert w\rVert^2 + \delta\,\pihat^\top w .
\]
Inserting $\delta w = b - \Abar y$ in both terms,
\[
\tfrac{\delta}{2}\lVert w\rVert^2 = \tfrac{1}{2\delta}\lVert\Abar y\rVert^2 - \tfrac{1}{\delta}b^\top\Abar y
  + \tfrac{1}{2\delta}\lVert b\rVert^2,\qquad
\delta\,\pihat^\top w = \pihat^\top b - \pihat^\top\Abar y .
\]
Adding $\cbar^\top y$ and collecting the terms that are linear in $y$ gives
$\cbar^\top y - (\pihat + b/\delta)^\top\Abar y = \ctil^\top y$, so the objective is
$f(y) + \frac{1}{2\delta}\lVert b\rVert^2 + \pihat^\top b$. The constraint~\eqref{eq:explicit-link}
only defines $w$, so the remaining constraints are $\Cbar y = \one$ and $y\ge0$. Since the
objectives differ by a constant, the two problems have the same minimizers.
\end{proof}

The Hessian of $f$ is $\frac{1}{\delta}\Abar^\top\Abar$, with the block structure
\begin{equation} \label{eq:hessian-blocks}
\frac{1}{\delta}\Abar^\top\Abar
= \frac{1}{\delta}\begin{bmatrix} A_E^\top A_E + A_I^\top A_I & -A_I^\top\\ -A_I & I\end{bmatrix}.
\end{equation}
It is positive semidefinite but in general singular: it has rank at most $m$, while the
number of variables $n + m_I$ grows with every generated column. This singularity is the main
difficulty for an active-set method, and it is addressed in
\cref{chap:nullspace,chap:singular}. Problem~\eqref{eq:eliminated} nevertheless has an
optimal solution, because $\lambda$ ranges over a bounded set and the objective grows
quadratically in $s$.

\subsection{The dual vector as a function of the primal iterate}

Relation~\eqref{eq:pi-w} with $w = (b-\Abar y)/\delta$ expresses the optimal dual through the
optimal $y$. The same expression is useful at \emph{every} $y$, which motivates the following
definition.

\begin{definition}[Dual estimate] \label{def:dual-estimate}
For $y = [\lambda; s]$ the \textbf{dual estimate} is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:pi-of-y}
\pi(y) = \pihat + \frac{1}{\delta}\bigl(b - \Abar y\bigr),
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
\begin{aligned}
\pi_E(y) &= \pihat_E + \tfrac{1}{\delta}\bigl(b_E - A_E\lambda\bigr),\\
\pi_I(y) &= \pihat_I + \tfrac{1}{\delta}\bigl(b_I - A_I\lambda + s\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

A surplus raises the dual of its row: an over-covered inequality row with a positive surplus
has a larger $\pi_i$ than it would have without the surplus. The next lemma is the central
identity of this work.

\begin{lemma}[Gradient--dual relation] \label{lem:gradient-dual}
For every $y$,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:gradient-dual}
\nabla f(y) = \cbar - \Abar^\top\pi(y),
\qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
\frac{\partial f}{\partial\lambda_j} = c_j - a_j^\top\pi(y), \qquad
\frac{\partial f}{\partial s_i} = \pi_i(y)\quad (i = 1,\dots,m_I),
\end{equation}
where $\pi_i$ denotes the dual of inequality row $i$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Differentiating $f(y) = \frac{1}{2\delta}y^\top\Abar^\top\Abar y + \ctil^\top y$ and
inserting~\eqref{eq:ctilde} for $\ctil$,
\[
\nabla f(y) = \tfrac{1}{\delta}\Abar^\top\Abar y + \cbar - \Abar^\top\pihat - \tfrac{1}{\delta}\Abar^\top b
= \cbar - \Abar^\top\Bigl(\pihat + \tfrac{1}{\delta}(b - \Abar y)\Bigr)
= \cbar - \Abar^\top\pi(y).
\]
The row of $\Abar^\top$ belonging to $\lambda_j$ is $a_j^\top$, and $\cbar_j = c_j$. The row
belonging to $s_i$ is $-1$ in the position of inequality row $i$ and zero elsewhere, and its
cost is $0$, which gives $0 - (-\pi_i) = \pi_i$.
\end{proof}

\Cref{lem:gradient-dual} has two consequences that are used throughout this work.
\begin{itemize}
    \item The gradient component of a column, $c_j - a_j^\top\pi$, is the \textbf{reduced cost
          of column $j$ without the convexity dual}. The gradient component of a surplus is the
          dual of its row. \Cref{chap:qpas} shows that this makes the optimality test of the
          master problem identical to the pricing test of column generation.
    \item The gradient, an $(n+m_I)$-vector whose length grows with every column, is
          determined by the $m$-vector $\pi(y)$. An implementation can store $\pi$ and
          evaluate the gradient only where it is needed.
\end{itemize}

The same idea gives the objective value. In the experiments of \cref{chap:results} the value
$\Phi(y)$ of~\eqref{eq:objective-relation} is reported as the \emph{master objective}; the
next proposition shows that it can be computed from $\pi$ without forming $\Abar y$ again.

\begin{prop}[Objective value] \label{prop:objective-value}
For every $y = [\lambda;s]$, with $\pi = \pi(y)$,
\[
\Phi(y) = c^\top\lambda + \frac{\delta}{2}\lVert\pi\rVert^2 - \frac{\delta}{2}\lVert\pihat\rVert^2 .
\]
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
By definition~\eqref{eq:objective-relation}, $\Phi(y) = \cbar^\top y + \frac{\delta}{2}\lVert w+\pihat\rVert^2
- \frac{\delta}{2}\lVert\pihat\rVert^2$ with $w = (b - \Abar y)/\delta$. Hence $w + \pihat = \pi(y)$
by~\eqref{eq:pi-of-y}, and $\cbar^\top y = c^\top\lambda$ because the surpluses have zero cost.
\end{proof}

Finally, the dual estimate measures the infeasibility of $y$ in the unregularized master
problem. This is the basis of the stopping criterion in \cref{sec:center-update}.

\begin{prop}[Residual and dual distance] \label{prop:residual}
For every $y$,
\[
b - \Abar y = \delta\bigl(\pi(y) - \pihat\bigr).
\]
In particular, $y$ satisfies the linking rows $\Abar y = b$ of the unified master
problem~\eqref{eq:unified} if and only if $\pi(y) = \pihat$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
Multiply the definition $\pi(y) = \pihat + \frac{1}{\delta}(b - \Abar y)$ by $\delta$ and
rearrange.
\end{proof}


\section{Regularized Column Generation}
\label{sec:center-update}

Column generation with the regularized master problem follows the classical scheme of
\cref{alg:classical-cg}, with two differences: the duals passed to the pricing problem are
the stabilized duals $\pi(y)$, and the stability center has to be updated.

\subsection{Center updates}

Two rules for the center update are common.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Proximal point rule:} after every master problem, $\pihat\gets\pi$. Every
          iteration is a step of the proximal point method on the current restricted dual
          problem.
    \item \textbf{Bundle rule:} $\pihat\gets\pi$ only if the Lagrangian bound improves,
          $\theta(\pi^+) > \theta(\pihat^+)$ with the projection $\pi^+$ of
          \cref{sec:bound-ineq} (a \emph{serious step}). Otherwise the center stays
          (a \emph{null step}). This requires the Lagrangian bound at every iteration, i.e.\
          all blocks must be priced every iteration. In an iteration without a new column, a
          null step would repeat the same master problem, so there the center is always
          moved (the center-only iteration of \cref{alg:rcg}).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Exact termination}

In classical column generation, the pricing test alone decides optimality
(\cref{prop:cg-optimality}). With regularization this is no longer true: when pricing finds
no improving column, the regularized master solution may still be infeasible for the
unregularized problem, since by \cref{prop:residual} $\Abar y\ne b$ as long as
$\pi\ne\pihat$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact termination] \label{thm:exact-termination}
Let $y = [\lambda; s]$ be optimal for the eliminated regularized master
problem~\eqref{eq:eliminated} over the columns $\mathcal{J}'$, with dual estimate
$\pi = \pi(y)$, and let $\mu$ be the Lagrange multipliers of the convexity
constraints~\eqref{eq:eliminated-conv}. If
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \label{thm:exact-termination:i} $\pi = \pihat$, and
    \item \label{thm:exact-termination:ii} the pricing problem~\eqref{eq:pricing} has a nonnegative optimal value for every
          block,
\end{enumerate}
then $\lambda$, extended by zeros, is optimal for the master problem~\eqref{eq:master}, and
$(\pi,\mu)$ is optimal for its dual~\eqref{eq:master-dual}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{Primal feasibility.} By \cref{prop:residual}, $\pi = \pihat$ gives $\Abar y = b$, i.e.\
$A_E\lambda = b_E$ and $A_I\lambda - s = b_I$. Since $s\ge0$, $A_I\lambda\ge b_I$, so $\lambda$
is feasible for~\eqref{eq:master}.

\emph{Dual feasibility.} The optimality conditions of~\eqref{eq:eliminated}, derived in
\cref{thm:kkt-master} of \cref{chap:qpas} (whose proof does not use this theorem), state that
\begin{itemize}
    \item $c_j - a_j^\top\pi - \mu_{\psi(j)}\ge0$ for every $j\in\mathcal{J}'$, with equality if
          $\lambda_j>0$;
    \item $\pi_i\ge0$ for every inequality row $i$, with equality if $s_i>0$.
\end{itemize}
Condition~\ref{thm:exact-termination:ii} extends the first inequality to all $j\in\mathcal{J}$, because the pricing
problem of each block minimizes $c_j - a_j^\top\pi - \mu_{\psi(j)}$ over its columns. Hence
$(\pi,\mu)$ satisfies both constraints of the dual~\eqref{eq:master-dual}.

\emph{Complementary slackness.} Positive weights have zero reduced cost by the first item. For
an inequality row, $\pi_i>0$ forces $s_i = 0$ by the second item, i.e.\ the row is satisfied
with equality. Feasible and complementary primal and dual solutions are optimal.
\end{proof}

If pricing finds no improving column while $\pi\ne\pihat$, the restricted master problem
contains the right columns for the moment, but the proximal term still keeps $\pi$ away from
the restricted dual optimum. The algorithm then moves the center to $\pi$ and solves the
master problem again \emph{without adding a column}. We call this a
\textbf{center-only iteration}. It is one proximal point step on the current restricted
model. As the duals move, pricing may find improving columns again. A run of regularized
column generation therefore consists of two kinds of iterations: iterations that add
columns, and center-only iterations that drive $\pi$ towards $\pihat$.
\Cref{prop:center-only} analyses the center-only iterations, and \cref{sec:results-phases}
measures both kinds.

\begin{prop}[Accuracy of the stopping test] \label{prop:stop-accuracy}
In practice condition~\ref{thm:exact-termination:i} of \cref{thm:exact-termination} is tested as
\begin{equation} \label{eq:center-test}
\lVert\pi - \pihat\rVert_\infty \;\le\; \varepsilon_{\mathrm{c}}\,\max\bigl(1,\lVert\pi\rVert_\infty\bigr)
\end{equation}
with a tolerance $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{c}}>0$. When this test holds, the residual of the
unified linking rows, and with it the violation of the linking rows by $\lambda$, is bounded by
\[
\max\Bigl(\lVert b_E - A_E\lambda\rVert_\infty,\;\lVert\max(b_I - A_I\lambda, 0)\rVert_\infty\Bigr)
\;\le\;\lVert b - \Abar y\rVert_\infty \;\le\; \delta\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{c}}\,\max\bigl(1,\lVert\pi\rVert_\infty\bigr).
\]
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{prop:residual}, $\lVert b - \Abar y\rVert_\infty = \delta\lVert\pi - \pihat\rVert_\infty$;
insert~\eqref{eq:center-test}. For the first inequality, $b - \Abar y = [\,b_E - A_E\lambda;\;
b_I - A_I\lambda + s\,]$, and since $s\ge0$, $\max(b_I - A_I\lambda, 0)\le|b_I - A_I\lambda + s|$
componentwise.
\end{proof}

The accuracy of the final solution is thus relative to the size of the duals, and it scales
with $\delta$. \Cref{sec:results-correctness,fig:optimality} show that the Lagrangian bound and the master
objective $\Phi$ are much less affected by this residual than the cost $c^\top\lambda$ of a
slightly infeasible $\lambda$.

\subsection{The Lagrangian bound with inequality rows}
\label{sec:bound-ineq}

The Lagrangian bound of \cref{prop:lagrangian-bound} requires $\pi_I\ge0$. The duals of the
regularized master problem satisfy this condition only up to the tolerance of the master
solver, and a tiny negative entry would void the guarantee $\theta(\pi)\le z^\star$
(\cref{rem:bound-sign}). The
bound is therefore evaluated at the projection
\[
\pi^+ = \bigl[\,\pi_E;\;\max(\pi_I, 0)\,\bigr],
\]
which always satisfies the sign condition, so that $\theta(\pi^+)\le z^\star$ holds by
\cref{prop:lagrangian-bound}. The same projected vector is passed to the pricing problem.
For problems with equality rows only, $\pi^+ = \pi$. At an exact optimum of the master problem
$\pi_I\ge0$ (\cref{thm:kkt-master}~\ref{thm:kkt-master:ii}), so $\pi^+ = \pi$ and the pricing test of
\cref{alg:rcg} is condition~\ref{thm:exact-termination:ii} of \cref{thm:exact-termination}.

\subsection{The algorithm}

\Cref{alg:rcg} collects the steps of this section. The first line of the loop, the solution
of the regularized master problem, is the subject of the remaining chapters.

\begin{algobox}{Regularized Column Generation}{alg:rcg}
\State \textbf{Input:} initial columns $\mathcal{J}'$ (one per block), center $\pihat$,
       parameter $\delta>0$, tolerances $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{c}}$, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rc}}$
\Repeat
    \State Solve the eliminated regularized master problem~\eqref{eq:eliminated}; obtain $y$,
           $\pi = \pi(y)$ and $\mu$
    \State $\pi^+\gets[\pi_E;\max(\pi_I,0)]$
    \State Price the blocks in random order with $(\pi^+,\mu)$; add the first column with
           $\bar c_j < -\varepsilon_{\mathrm{rc}}$ to $\mathcal{J}'$
    \If{no column was added (all blocks priced)}
        \State $\theta\gets\theta(\pi^+)$ \Comment{valid lower bound on $z^\star$}
        \If{$\lVert\pi-\pihat\rVert_\infty\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{c}}\max(1,\lVert\pi\rVert_\infty)$}
            \State \textbf{stop}: $\lambda$ is optimal up to the accuracy of \cref{prop:stop-accuracy}
        \EndIf
        \State $\pihat\gets\pi$ \Comment{center-only iteration}
    \Else
        \State $\pihat\gets\pi$ \Comment{proximal point rule}
    \EndIf
\Until{stopped}
\end{algobox}

The bundle rule would need the Lagrangian bound in every iteration and hence full pricing; the
experiments of \cref{chap:results} use the proximal point rule.


\section{An Adaptive Stabilization Parameter}
\label{sec:adaptive}

\Cref{alg:rcg} keeps $\delta$ fixed. This section shows that $\delta$ is the step size of a
proximal point method on the restricted dual function, recalls what the theory of that method
says about variable step sizes, and derives a simple rule that changes $\delta$ during the
run. Its effect is measured in \cref{sec:results-adaptive}.

\subsection{The stabilization parameter as a step size}

For a set $\mathcal{J}'$ of columns, write $\mathcal{J}'_k = \mathcal{J}'\cap\mathcal{J}_k$ and
let
\[
D(\pi) = b^\top\pi + \sum_{k=1}^{K}\,\min_{j\in\mathcal{J}'_k}\bigl(c_j - a_j^\top\pi\bigr)
\qquad\text{for } \pi_I\ge0,
\]
and $D(\pi) = -\infty$ otherwise. This is the objective of the restricted
dual~\eqref{eq:restricted-dual} with $\mu$ maximized out, since the best $\mu_k$ is the
smallest $c_j - a_j^\top\pi$ of block $k$. $D$ is concave and piecewise linear, and, since
$\mathcal{J}'\subseteq\mathcal{J}$, it overestimates the Lagrangian bound,
$D(\pi)\ge\theta(\pi)$ (\cref{prop:lagrangian-bound}): $D$ is the cutting-plane model of
$\theta$ given by the current columns. A vector $g$ is a \textbf{supergradient} of $D$ at $\pi$
if $D(\pi')\le D(\pi) + g^\top(\pi'-\pi)$ for all $\pi'$; the set of supergradients is
$\partial D(\pi)$.

\begin{prop}[Proximal step] \label{prop:proximal-step}
Let $y$ be optimal for the eliminated regularized master problem~\eqref{eq:eliminated} with
center $\pihat$ and parameter $\delta$, and let $\pi = \pi(y)$. Then
\[
\pi = \arg\max_{\pi'}\; D(\pi') - \frac{\delta}{2}\lVert\pi' - \pihat\rVert^2
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\pi = \pihat + \frac{1}{\delta}\,r, \quad r = b - \Abar y\in\partial D(\pi).
\]
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
By \cref{thm:explicit-duality,thm:eliminated}, $\pi$ is the $\pi$-part of the optimal solution
of the stabilized dual~\eqref{eq:stabilized-dual}. Maximizing over $\mu$ first gives the
stated problem, whose objective is strictly concave, so $\pi$ is its unique maximizer. Its
optimality condition is $0\in\partial D(\pi) - \delta(\pi - \pihat)$, i.e.\
$\delta(\pi-\pihat)\in\partial D(\pi)$, and $\delta(\pi - \pihat) = b - \Abar y$ by
\cref{prop:residual}.
\end{proof}

A master solve is therefore an implicit supergradient step of length $1/\delta$ on the
restricted dual, and a sequence of center-only iterations is the proximal point method
\cite{rockafellar1976monotone} on the fixed function $D$, with step parameters
$c_t = 1/\delta_t$. Its classical theory makes the role of the step size precise.

\begin{remark}[Proximal point method with variable steps] \label{rem:prox-theory}
Let $\Pi^\star$ be the set of maximizers of $D$, assumed nonempty.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The iterates converge to a point of $\Pi^\star$ for every sequence with
          $\sum_t c_t = \infty$, in particular for every sequence with $\delta_t\le\delta_0$
          \cite{rockafellar1976monotone, guler1991convergence}.
    \item The values satisfy $D^\star - D(\pi_t)\le \operatorname{dist}(\pi_0,\Pi^\star)^2 /
          (2\sum_{s<t}c_s)$ \cite{guler1991convergence}. With a fixed $\delta$ the gap decreases
          like $\delta/t$; with $\delta_t = \delta_0\gamma^t$, $\gamma<1$, it decreases
          geometrically.
    \item Since $D$ is polyhedral, the method reaches $\Pi^\star$ after finitely many steps
          for every sequence $c_t$ bounded away from zero
          \cite{rockafellar1976monotone, ferris1991finite}.
    \item For a linear program there is a $\bar\delta(\pihat)>0$ such that for every
          $\delta\le\bar\delta(\pihat)$ the proximal point is the projection of $\pihat$ onto
          $\Pi^\star$ \cite{mangasarian1979nonlinear, friedlander2007exact}: a small enough
          $\delta$ reaches $\Pi^\star$ in one step.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

On a fixed restricted model, a small $\delta$ is therefore always better. With a fixed
$\delta$, the center-only iterations walk towards $\Pi^\star$ in steps of length
$\lVert r\rVert/\delta$, and once the working set has settled the step does not change from
one iteration to the next (\cref{prop:center-only}); the number of such iterations grows
with $\delta$. This is the long proximal phase observed in \cref{sec:results-phases}. A small
$\delta$ throughout is no solution either: when new columns enter, the model changes, and a
small $\delta$ lets the duals jump like the duals of the linear master problem, which is the
instability that the regularization is meant to remove (\cref{rem:instability}). The
parameter should be small while the model is fixed and large when it changes.

\subsection{The geometric rule}

\begin{definition}[Geometric stabilization parameter] \label{def:geometric-delta}
Let $\delta_0>0$ be the base value and $0<\gamma<1\le\beta$, $0<\delta_{\min}\le\delta_0$.
After every iteration of \cref{alg:rcg} in which the center moves, the parameter of the next
master problem is
\begin{equation} \label{eq:geometric-delta}
\delta \;\gets\;
\begin{cases}
\max(\delta_{\min},\ \gamma\,\delta) & \text{after a center-only iteration},\\
\min(\delta_0,\ \beta\,\delta) & \text{after an iteration that adds a column}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

During a run of center-only iterations the parameter follows the geometric sequence
$\delta_0\gamma^t$, which gives the rule its name; we use $\gamma = 1/2$ and $\beta = 2$. The
choice $\beta = \infty$ resets $\delta$ to $\delta_0$ whenever a column is added. In the
column phase, where almost every iteration adds a column, $\delta$ stays at $\delta_0$, so
the new columns enter under full stabilization as in \cref{alg:rcg}. The rule needs no
information beyond whether pricing added a column, so it works with first-improving pricing
and does not require the Lagrangian bound.

\begin{prop}[Properties of the geometric rule] \label{prop:geometric-delta}
Let $\delta$ be changed by~\eqref{eq:geometric-delta}, or by any other rule, between two
master problems.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \label{prop:geometric-delta:i} The change leaves the iterate feasible, and for the
          active-set method of the following
          chapters it leaves the ordering, the nullspace basis and the factorization
          unchanged. Only the dual estimate changes, to $\pihat + (b - \Abar y)/\delta$.
    \item \label{prop:geometric-delta:ii} \Cref{thm:exact-termination} holds unchanged, and
          \cref{prop:stop-accuracy} holds with the value of $\delta$ in the last master
          problem.
\end{enumerate}
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
(i) The constraints~\eqref{eq:eliminated-conv} and~\eqref{eq:eliminated-bounds} do not contain
$\delta$. For the active-set method, $\Heff$ does not depend on $\delta$
(\cref{rem:factor-independent}); $\delta$ enters only the right-hand side of the reduced
system and the dual estimate~\eqref{eq:pi-of-y}, exactly like a new center
(\cref{thm:hot-start}~\ref{thm:hot-start:ii}).
(ii) Neither the statement nor the proof of \cref{thm:exact-termination} uses the value of
$\delta$, and the proof of \cref{prop:stop-accuracy} uses only \cref{prop:residual} for the
last master problem.
\end{proof}

Part~\ref{prop:geometric-delta:i} is what makes the rule attractive for this method: a general
quadratic programming solver sees a new Hessian $\frac{1}{\delta}\Abar^\top\Abar$ after every
change of $\delta$ and has to refactorize it, whereas for the active-set method a change of
$\delta$ costs as little as a new center. Part~\ref{prop:geometric-delta:ii} shows a second
benefit: if the run ends in the proximal phase, the final $\delta$ is much smaller than
$\delta_0$, and so is the bound on the residual of the linking rows.

\medskip

The rule is a simple form of the \emph{proximity control} of bundle methods
\cite{kiwiel1990proximity}, which adapts $1/\delta$ to the progress of the method. A
center-only iteration brings no new information into the model, so a longer step is safe; a
new column changes the model, and the step is shortened again. Stabilized column generation
uses related schedules for the parameters of penalty, box and smoothing stabilization
\cite{dumerle1999stabilized, benamor2009choice, pessoa2018automation}. Rules that compare the
actual increase of $\theta$ with the increase predicted by $D$ come with convergence guarantees
for the bundle method, but they need the Lagrangian bound in every iteration and hence full
pricing.
